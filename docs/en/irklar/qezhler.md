# Qezh

<p class="lore-lead">The Qezh treat their desert wrappings as a second skin against heat, and their form as a language of family, mourning, status, and belonging.</p>

<div class="creature-layout"><section class="creature-statblock"><h2>The language of desert wrappings</h2><p class="creature-type">Avarra-original people · one of Qasyrra’s three core communities</p><div class="creature-section"><h3>Daylight and the body</h3><p>Layered cloth is not an aesthetic costume. It guards against sand, dryness, and the Sun. An uncovered face is intimate; the colour, layer, and tying of a wrap can communicate house, mourning period, marriage bond, or a particular right of passage.</p></div><div class="creature-section"><h3>Death and refuge</h3><p>Qezh traditions strongly shape the funerary side of Neshar ritual. Preserving the body, cooling the grave, and carrying the names of those left behind matter as much as death itself. This does not mean every Qezh belongs to the priesthood.</p></div><div class="creature-section"><h3>Diaspora</h3><p>Qezh communities did not disappear when foreign trade opened, but commonly formed more guarded networks of kinship and hospitality. A Qezh house in a port is not a single “desert quarter”; it may be an intersection of different households bound by mourning, travel, and water debt.</p></div></section><aside class="creature-profile"><div class="portrait-placeholder has-image"><img src="/assets/illustrations/qezh.png" alt="Representative Qezh portrait" /></div></aside></div>

## Place in Avarra

Qezh live beyond the [Qasyrra Empire](../devletler/qasyrra-imparatorlugu), but the empire’s interior traditions of wrapping, burial, and water right remain their strongest shared ground. A Qezh person’s caste, house, Neshar ties, and travel history say more than the people-name alone.

## Adventure ties

- A mourning wrap’s knot tells which water route a missing traveller entered.
- A Qezh guest house agrees to shelter a foreign caravan in return for testimony on an old water debt.
