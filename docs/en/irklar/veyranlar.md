# Veyrans

<p class="lore-lead">Veyrans are a people of wholly humanoid appearance, known for double-ringed irises and fine natural lines on their hands. Being the founding people of Ordan does not mean that all Veyrans support the empire.</p>

## Source

Veyrans are not equivalent to a published D&D player people. They are an original people created for Avarra. They can be used for a humanoid player character, but their identity is not a subtype of a lineage from the Player’s Handbook.

## Formation: the Seven Lines Refuge

Veyrans are the many-generational inheritance of communities founded in Asteron’s [Seven Lines Refuge](../tarih/seven-lines-refuge) after the Vein collapse that began around 2640 BC. Long life amid the refuge’s water, stone, and unstable magical fields made inherited traits such as double-ringed irises and fine lines more apparent. This is not an experiment, curse, or chosenness; it is the natural, ongoing history of a people formed in disaster.

The refuge culture first aimed not to confine people in records, but not to forget those who were missing or in need. Ordan later transformed the principles of measure and record into imperial ideology. Witness, rotation of duty, and correction of false records remain living principles in many Veyran communities.

## Bodily inheritance and kinship

Veyran traits include two nested iris rings, fine natural lines on the neck and hands, and sensitivity to shifts in light. Appearance varies from person to person, and traits may appear with different intensity in mixed families. None says anything about intelligence, morality, profession, or a right to rule. Veyran is ancestry; being a Meridian Veyran, Border Veyran, or Unlined is a matter of history, place, and upbringing.

## Cultural distinction

Meridian Veyrans consider record and measure sacred. Border Veyrans are entwined with local cultures. The Unlined rise through examination bureaucracy against old noble laws, while Anti-Order movements argue that the empire is killing nature.

## Canonical record

<p>Veyrans have fully humanoid appearance. Their skin may be grey, copper, dark olive, or pale bronze. Their eyes hold two nested iris rings, while fine natural lines mark neck and hands.</p>
<p>Veyrans founded Ordan, but not all Veyrans are pro-imperial.</p>

## Major cultures

<div class="lore-grid"><div class="lore-card"><h3>Meridian Veyrans</h3><p>The capital culture that treats written law, examination, and measurement as the foundation of civilisation.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Border Veyrans</h3><p>Communities born in conquered regions and shaped by local languages and mixed families.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>The Unlined</h3><p>Veyrans whose lineage is considered weak, excluded from old nobility but able to rise in the bureaucracy.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Anti-Order Movements</h3><p>Rural and religious movements that argue that binding the world into records is suffocating Avarra.</p></div></div>

<p class="lore-callout">Families treat their children’s education and examination success as shared standing. Losing documents is not merely an administrative problem, but a loss of social identity.</p>

## Player-character ties

- A Veyran character can choose which Seven Lines principle their family treats as inheritance: witness, measure, rotation, or correction.
- Being Ordan is never mandatory. A border village, mixed neighbourhood, water council, migrant guild, or Anti-Order circle is an equally valid start.
- The empire’s “chosen founding people” story can be a character’s belief, rejected propaganda, or family conflict. It is not biological fact.
