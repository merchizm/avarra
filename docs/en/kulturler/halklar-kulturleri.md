# Guide to Peoples and Cultures

<p class="lore-lead">In Avarra, ancestry, culture, citizenship, and social standing are not the same thing. A people’s bodily or magical inheritance does not by itself determine its values or the law to which it belongs.</p>

<div class="lore-grid"><div class="lore-card"><h3>Ancestry</h3><p>Human, Elf, Dwarf, Veyran, or a transformational mark: a matter of body and supernatural inclination.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Culture</h3><p>Learned values and daily life, such as Woodborn, Meridian Veyran, or a Road Hearth.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Citizenship</h3><p>Legal belonging to a polity such as the Thorn Crown, Ordan, or River Throne.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Social standing</h3><p>How someone is seen in the world: noble, refugee, unregistered, priest, or worker.</p></div></div>

## Cultural currents

<div class="lore-grid"><div class="lore-card"><h3>Humans and Veyrans</h3><p>Old Crowned, Woodborn, Road Hearths, Imperial Humans; Meridian Veyrans, Border Veyrans, and the Unlined.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Elves and Fey peoples</h3><p>Forest treaties, Lethar’s star order, sea kinships, Veil traditions, and seasonal identities.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Stone and smaller peoples</h3><p>Hearth law, lantern cities, guild nights, and tunnel labour. No single state explains any of them.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Lineage marks</h3><p>Tiefling, Aasimar, Reborn, Hexblood, and Dhampir are usually not separate nations, but conditions and ancestral houses found across many cultures.</p></div></div>

<p class="lore-callout">No morality belongs to an ancestry. Members of the same people may be citizens of rival states, followers of different faiths, or positioned in opposed social classes.</p>

## Canonical cultural principles

## 1. The four layers of identity

| Layer | Question | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Ancestry | What are they bodily or magically? | Human, Elf, Dwarf, Veyran |
| Culture | With which values were they raised? | Woodborn, Meridian Veyran |
| Citizenship | Which law binds them? | Thorn Crown, Ordan |
| Social standing | How are they seen in the world? | Noble, refugee, unregistered, priest |

Tiefling, Aasimar, Reborn, Hexblood, and Dhampir are usually ancestral or transformational conditions rather than cultures. A human Aasimar and a dwarf Aasimar may share a sacred sign without sharing a culture.

Genasi are not one nation either. Elemental ancestry can appear within many peoples; in regions such as Camova and the River Throne, concentrations have formed distinct cultures.

## 56. Mixed families

Families between peoples are common in Avarra, although not every pairing produces biological children. Family law can be formed independently of biology through adoption, hearth bonds, road partnership, and name treaties.

Terms such as Half-Elf and Half-Orc are bodily descriptions in some regions and crude outside labels in others. A person’s culture is shaped by family and where they were raised.

## 57. Language canon

“Common” is not one natural world language. It is a language family simplified by trade, Ordan bureaucracy, and intercontinental road networks. Local languages will be detailed in a separate canon record.

Principal language families include human crown languages; Veyran and Asteron languages; Elvish dialects and Sylvan; Dwarvish and ancient hearth scripts; Nimrûn; Goblinoid languages; Draconic and Odrakan languages; Aquan-derived water languages; and Underground Common.

## 58. No ancestry-bound morality

No people is born good, evil, lawful, or chaotic. Alignment in stat blocks describes an individual or the institution they represent. Cultures can create behavioural pressure; biology does not create moral destiny.

## 59. Canon scope check

This edition names every living people, lineage condition, and ancient sapient people explicitly present in Avarra World Atlas 0.1. It includes humans and Veyrans; Elven and Fey peoples; Firbolgs, Thornbloods, Satyrs; Dwarves, Duergar, Gnomes, Nimrûn, Halflings, Goblinoids; elemental lineages; Dragonborn and other Odrakan peoples; lineage marks; deep peoples; sea peoples; constructed peoples; Yuan-ti, Nagas, and sealed Mind Flayer remnants; and Dragons, Giants, Fomorians, Aboleths, and First Fey beings.

“Various humanoids” is not a people name. When a new D&D people enters Avarra, this scope list, the culture section, and its representative stat block must be updated together.
