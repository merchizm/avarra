    <h1>A soul is not<br>one thing.</h1>
    <p>Most Avarran traditions explain a person’s death through three elements: <b>Breath</b>, <b>Name</b>, and <b>Trace</b>. This is not the fixed dogma of one sect, but a shared language through which healers, mourners, jurists, and rite-workers can understand one another.</p>
    <div class="soul-cover-facts"><span><b>Ⅰ</b>Breath · vitality</span><span><b>Ⅱ</b>Name · personhood</span><span><b>Ⅲ</b>Trace · residue</span></div>
  </header>

  <section class="soul-section">
    <div class="soul-section-head"><p class="lore-kicker">Three elements</p><h2>What separates in death?</h2><p>The distinction explains why a returned body is not automatically the same person, why a ghost is not always a soul, and why a funeral concerns more than a body.</p></div>
    <div class="soul-triad">
      <article class="soul-card breath"><span>Ⅰ</span><h3>Breath</h3><p>The force that keeps a body alive: heat, movement, metabolism, and the urge to live. After death it enters the wider cycle; by itself, it is neither memory, will, nor person.</p><small>The language of healers and Living Whole readings</small></article>
      <article class="soul-card name"><span>Ⅱ</span><h3>Name</h3><p>The whole identity made of will, memory, choice, and selfhood. A true resurrection must find not merely a body or Breath, but the Name that chooses to return.</p><small>The language of grief, inheritance, and consent law</small></article>
      <article class="soul-card trace"><span>Ⅲ</span><h3>Trace</h3><p>The emotional and magical residue of a life: habit, fear, love, an image embedded in a place, or a repeated sound. A powerful Trace may behave like a spirit; not every ghost is a true Name.</p><small>The language of dreams, shadows, and place-records</small></article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="soul-section soul-section--flow">
    <div class="soul-section-head"><p class="lore-kicker">The course of passage</p><h2>Three tasks in one death</h2></div>
    <div class="soul-flow">
      <article><span>01</span><h3>Secure the body</h3><p>When Breath ceases, a healer first establishes bodily death and protects the body and those around it. This is not yet a judgment about the soul.</p></article>
      <article><span>02</span><h3>Release the Name</h3><p>Funeral words let a Name pass without being tied to the living’s debt. Many traditions therefore record last wishes, unfinished work, and inheritance separately.</p></article>
      <article><span>03</span><h3>Place the Trace</h3><p>Grave, mask, memory stone, table, or burnt letter each helps a Trace be remembered without harming the community. The aim is not forgetting, but refusing to turn a residue into a person.</p></article>
    </div>
  </section>

  <section class="soul-section soul-section--split">
    <div class="soul-prose"><p class="lore-kicker">Legal consequence</p><h2>A return does not mean every debt returns with it.</h2><p>When a person is resurrected, Avarra’s laws do not all reach the same answer. Some cities find the Name’s consent sufficient; others reopen marriage, property, and military oaths for negotiation. The doctrine matters here: “the body moves again” and “the same person has returned” are not the same statement.</p><p>Likewise, a speaking ghost cannot automatically serve as a witness. If only a Trace speaks, it may carry a true memory without carrying the whole person or free will. Threshold traditions therefore ask that words from the dead be verified against living records.</p></div>
    <aside class="soul-questions"><p class="lore-kicker">Open questions</p><div><b>How long can a Trace last?</b><span>It changes with place, attachment, and magical force.</span></div><div><b>Where does a Name go?</b><span>Faith families differ; the common language names no certain destination.</span></div><div><b>Can a soul divide?</b><span>Most teachings say a Name cannot, though a Trace may remain in many places.</span></div></aside>
  </section>
