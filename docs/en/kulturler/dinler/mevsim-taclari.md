# Seasonal Crowns

<p class="lore-lead">Seasonal Crowns teaches that divine faces change with the seasons, and that change itself is sacred. A person, city, or office remaining exactly the same is not necessarily virtue; it can be a refusal of its season.</p>

## Four faces, one responsibility

The winter face may mourn, the spring face forgive, the summer face fight, and the autumn face judge. These may be read as distinct gods or as states of one power through time. Change is not hypocrisy to the Seasonal Crowns; the real question is how one answers for the promise made before changing.

At each turn, small crowns are braided and then unmade. Spring crowns hold shoots and pale thread; summer crowns marks of labour; autumn crowns lists of debt; winter crowns names of loss. The rite does not choose a new identity. It lets the community witness what must be put down. It is strong among Eladrin communities without belonging to Elves alone.

## The right to change

The Crowns defend transformation in family, work, and citizenship. A warrior becoming a mourner, a judge admitting error, or an exile taking root in a new district are sacred crossings in its language. Critics call the tradition unstable; its adherents answer that unchanging offices often only evade responsibility.

The faith does not romanticise speed. A crown is unmade only when its season ends, and a powerful person’s declaration of change does not erase the damage they left. An order given in summer requires an account in autumn.
