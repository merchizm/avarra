# Root Return Lineages

<p class="lore-lead">For the Root Return lineages, rebirth is not the tale of a lone soul rising into the sky. Name echoes in households of care, Trace in shared work, and Breath around the same hearth.</p>

Their centre is not blood purity but **sustained care**. An adopted child, a long-kept apprentice, or an orphan raised in the same funeral house can be bound as deeply to a household root as a birth heir. Root Return can therefore look conservative while providing some of the strongest traditions of adoption and orphan protection.

## Root ledgers

Lineage houses record not just births but who cared for whom, who forgave which debt, and who carried which dead. These ledgers serve inheritance cases, but more deeply they are ways of asking where a Name might echo. A rebirth claim does not automatically confer a right: the child must speak freely, two witnesses outside the family must record the case, and no old debt may be forced upon them. The last rule is the one most often broken.

Root Return accepts that a Name can echo in the body of another people. That possibility turns every such claim into a political mine: a human noble’s Trace in a Drow worker, an elven scholar’s Name in a Goblin child, or a sea captain’s responsibility in a landborn descendant.

At the Root Table, places are set for the living and recently dead; the dead receive no meal, while the names of those who cared for them are read aloud. Critics warn that ledgers become closed-house power. Some cities therefore require every ledger to be read to the quarter assembly once each year.
