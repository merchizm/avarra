# Mareya’s Way of Well and Mercy

<p class="lore-lead">Mareya’s first ruling is that the thirsty person’s claim comes before the debt ledger. The Way of Well and Mercy treats water not only as a resource, but as the measure of law, care, and mercy.</p>

Mareyans bind wells, fountains, hospitals, road houses, and debt courts into one religious network. Delaying a debt, giving a patient clean water, or housing a traveller for three nights are not only good deeds but public forms of divine mercy.

The way spread especially through dense human cities. Its strength carries a fear: mercy distributed in whose name? Some shrines are accused of demanding declaration, political loyalty, or a child’s surrender to schooling in return for aid. Reformers answer that mercy with a price is not mercy.

Mareya’s symbol is an unlocked but measured bowl. It offers water to everyone, while making visible the shared duty to protect the source.
