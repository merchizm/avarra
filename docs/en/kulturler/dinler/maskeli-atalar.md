# Masked Ancestors

<p class="lore-lead">Masked Ancestors invites the spirit or memory of an ancestor into a prepared mask for a bounded rite. Its purpose is not to take orders from the dead, but to hear the memory they carry without turning them into an instrument.</p>

## Preparing the mask

A mask is not a copy of a face. It bears a known gesture, unfinished work, beloved material, or mistake that deserves telling. The maker hears the living family’s account while working, so masks can be as contested as family archives. If no one can speak honestly of an ancestor, the mask is delayed.

During the rite, the bearer does not use their own name. Those present address them not through old family hierarchy but as “guest.” Afterwards the mask is broken, burned, or carefully cleansed. Wearing it too long can mix a person’s identity with ancestral memory; this taboo is also a practical boundary protecting the grieving.

## Memory, inheritance, consent

The practice is especially used among dynastic, craft, and migrant families in Odraka. A mask is not direct proof in an inheritance dispute: the living must verify a word heard in rite through their own records. The dead are not allowed to become the final voice over the living.

The tradition has sometimes been stained by spectacle, blackmail, and false claims of lineage. Reliable rites therefore record the bearer’s consent, the mask’s making chain, and its final cleansing. Its force lies less in whether a mask literally speaks than in the honesty it requires from those who remember.
