# Kezran’s Returning Judgement

<p class="lore-lead">Kezran’s promise is less a date than an accusation: this order is not the final order. Judgement will return one day, but waiting and trying to force it are not the same act.</p>

Returning Judgement grew around the idea of a lost ruler, expected deliverer, apocalypse, and historical transformation. Some branches wait quietly. Some prepare the world through opposition to debt, violence, and injustice. Radicals treat hastening collapse as a duty.

Kezrani movements become political rapidly during occupation, scarcity, and succession crisis. A child, general, or exiled leader claimed to be the awaited figure becomes a question of army and crown, not only sect. When the expected day passes, the faith does not vanish: the count was wrong, transformation began invisibly, or the people were not prepared.

Its greatest fear is its own prophecy. Every Kezrani must ask whether one speaking for the deliverer truly begins transformation, or merely wishes to burn the present world.
