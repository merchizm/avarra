# Silent Table

<p class="lore-lead">Silent Table is a hospitality custom that leaves an empty place for the dead on certain nights. Its purpose is not to call the dead, but to be ready to receive them without commanding them should they return.</p>

## The rule of the empty place

A table leaves a plate, cup, or simply an empty stool turned toward the door. No question is asked of the dead; no news or service is demanded. Families may speak around a loss, but do not address the empty place directly. The silence is not fear. It lets the other remain a person.

The rite often accompanies anniversaries, first harvest, return from a long journey, or the aftermath of war. It is common among Halflings, rural human communities, and mixed urban districts, though it changes from home to home. Some leave salt at a threshold; some send the meal to a neighbour. The shared principle is that grief is not sealed inside a single family.

## Making room for the living

Silent Table matters outside funerals too. Leaving a place for a lonely neighbour, an absent traveller, or an unnamed war dead keeps the missing visible to a community. Migrant associations and neighbourhood kitchens in cities therefore adopt the practice easily.

Some critics say it binds people to the past. Those who keep the table answer the opposite: making room for the dead prevents the living from becoming the owner of that place. The meal ends, the plate is cleared, and life continues.
