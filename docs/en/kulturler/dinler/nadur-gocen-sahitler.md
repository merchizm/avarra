# Nadûr’s Wandering Witnesses

<p class="lore-lead">Nadûr entrusted the word not to a stone wall but to a walking tongue. For the Wandering Witnesses, a text survives not because of its ink, but because it can be spoken correctly beside a well.</p>

This way lives among nomadic human communities. Route, well, herd, and season of mourning form its religious calendar. Oral lineage memory replaces written genealogy; an utterer may recite an ancestor’s name, a water debt, and an old revelation line in the same evening.

Settled shrine historians dismiss the Witnesses as “debased” or “primitive.” Their members reject that language: to fix the text is to forget that God walked with an exiled people. Long exclusion, closed marriage networks, and scarcity have nevertheless narrowed many communities.

Pouring the first water into a well, naming a dead traveller one last time on the route, and giving shade to a stranger are major rites. The poisoning of three wells along one route is therefore not only displacement; in Nadûri law, it is breach of covenant.
