# Broken Circle Teaching

<p class="lore-lead">The Broken Circle says that some lives are not a natural cycle, but a prison repeated through debt, bondage, dynasty, and forced soul-binding. No release comes until the circle is broken.</p>

It spread among people torn from their households by debt after great floods and famines. Its first speakers did not promise rebirth. If a person was expected to live again under the same master, with the same debt and hunger, that was not cosmic order but living oppression.

## What it means to break

Breaking does not mean cutting every bond. It means recovering one’s Name, labour, and body; ensuring a child is not born under ancestral debt; and refusing to hold a dead person by force. The path forbids selling a body or child for debt, coercive soul-binding, and using a birth claim to rule over a person’s free will.

## The violence dispute

Quiet circles read debt ledgers in public, organise strikes, escape lines, and mutual aid. Hard circles seize temple stores, empty slave camps by force, and burn dynastic records. Thus the same teaching may be remembered as a refuge house or a rebel cell. Silent Current monks fear that this danger exposes every care institution; Circle speakers answer that monastic neutrality often protects wealthy order.

Resurrection is especially fraught. A willing return can be discussed; forcing Breath into a body for money, lineage, or war is among the gravest circle crimes. No central authority governs all Circles. They are joined by a single question: **“If suffering is not a debt from a past life, whose hand holds it today?”**
