# Whispering Crossroads

<p class="lore-lead">The Whispering Crossroads turns Theskar’s saying “the dead do not vanish” upside down: instead of accompanying the dead onward, it claims it can make their remaining Trace speak.</p>

Its founders said they wanted to help families of those lost in war. Through a ring, bone fragment, last letter, or soil from the death place, they claimed to hear a dead person’s voice. A rite may seem to reveal something unknown; sometimes a grieving family hears exactly what it needed to hear. The distinction is almost impossible to make from outside.

## A Trace held at the Threshold

Mainstream Theskar teaches that the living should not follow a person into the Threshold or block their road. The Crossroads claims a part of Trace can be bound and summoned for useful ends: lost inheritances, hidden murder, war orders, or unburied debts. The promise is powerful.

But the voice may not be the dead person at all. It could be an echo, a Threshold entity, a mage-guided sound, or grief speaking with its own voice. Members preserve this uncertainty as “depth of mystery”; critics say it estranges a person from both their dead and themselves.

Crossroads cells grow where grief remains unclosed. A “last conversation” may cost money, service, a secret, or a new recruit. Some use a dead merchant’s supposed Trace to steer inheritance; others bind young people through the name of a war loss. Their gravest practice is using living people not as mediums but as **vessels**—weakening their sense of self amid mourning objects and voices. To Theskarites, this is not only necromancy but forcing a living person toward the Threshold.

When a cell is exposed, grief houses record every dead name invoked, what each family gave, and which words changed inheritance or judgement. Even a voice proved false may have left real harm behind.
