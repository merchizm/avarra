# Eshrenic Faith

<p class="lore-lead">Eshrenics believe the world was not made evil. It has been wounded, and what wounds it is not only monsters or disaster, but the lie spoken knowingly, the harm abandoned, and the pain a person refuses to repair.</p>

For an Eshrenic, the most terrible phrase is not “the gods willed it.” It is **“it has always been this way.”** The Corrupting Will grows precisely there: when it convinces people that repair is impossible, that the powerful need not answer, and that another person’s pain is normal.

The faith therefore asks for no passive obedience. Cleaning a well, making a lie public through testimony, protecting civilians in war, burying an unclaimed dead person, repairing a bridge, or standing beside a debt-crushed household are all cosmic work.

## Eshren’s word

Eshren’s original name is unknown. The earliest texts call them a teacher, water-worker, healer, or simply **the One Who Saw the Wound**. During a great plague and water blight, they opposed a local priesthood demanding more sacrifice. Every tradition preserves their answer with small changes:

> The world does not ask you for sacrifice. The world asks you to close the wound.

What persuaded believers was not fire falling from the sky. It was that Eshren’s followers opened clean-water channels, buried the dead, forced granaries open, and repaired the places suffering most. The faith understands miracle not as command, but as **right action still having force**.

## Founding Breath and Corrupting Will

Eshrenics do not speak of two equal gods. The **Founding Breath** is the first will that made the world capable of life, relation, and repair. The **Corrupting Will** is not a rival creator; it is a spoiling force that feeds on fear, openings in order, and harm done knowingly.

Corruption is not every sickness, poverty, or natural disaster. To read it that way would blame those who suffer. It is found in the hand that hoards water after disaster, the order that forbids burial, the pen writing a false record, and the tongue saying “there was no other choice.” In some regions it can still leave physical marks on magic, soil, and memory: colourless water, a quarter forgetting the same name, repeated murder, or a shadow-rift that will not heal.

## Three vows

  <article class="lore-card"><h3>True Word</h3><p>A lie—especially one that protects power—opens a door to the Corrupting Will. Testimony is sacred work.</p></article>
  <article class="lore-card"><h3>Clean Labour</h3><p>Do not foul water, land, bodies, knowledge, or work at another’s expense. Do not flee what your work leaves behind.</p></article>
  <article class="lore-card"><h3>Debt of Repair</h3><p>Regret is incomplete if it gives nothing to the harmed. Where possible, harm must be repaired.</p></article>
</div>

These vows do not demand one life from every Eshrenic. A farmer may protect soil, a healer a patient, a clerk the record, a soldier civilians, and a merchant the vulnerable side of a contract. The teaching asks not for flight from a craft, but responsibility for the wound a craft can open.

## Work, not worship alone

Eshrenic shrines have lamps, prayer, and song, but none is sufficient alone. A great feast is incomplete if wells have not been openly inspected, if a funeral house has not settled its debts, or if beds have not been set aside for those without shelter.

- **Wound Reckoning:** A settlement publicly records hidden debts, poisoned sources, unburied dead, and unresolved harms.
- **First Water Vow:** No celebration begins before clean water is set aside for children and the sick.
- **Ash and Seed:** At a funeral, ash goes to earth and seed to the living; a concrete repair is undertaken in the dead person’s name.
- **Open Testimony:** An office-holder tells the public once a year what harm they caused and how they repaired it.

## Paths and deviations

**Repairers** form the broad current of healers, farmers, teachers, and mediators. **Open Witnesses** demand that king, guild, and priesthood answer publicly. **Ash Wardens** accept firmer protection against magical corruption, bondage, poisoning, and organised violence.

The deepest wound opens within: **Purifiers** claim corruption roots in certain peoples, the sick, or users of magic. The main faith names this heresy. No person is born corruption; destroying someone in the name of saving the world is one of the Corrupting Will’s easiest victories.

## The ash orders

Ash is not annihilation to Eshrenics; it is proof that something burned and can no longer be hidden. The Eshrenic world has many independent Ash orders. The [Ash Knights](./kul-sovalyeleri) are one such order, sworn to protect those left after war, plague, and magical rifts. Their island home, [Ash Threshold](../../yerlesimler/kul-esigi), accepts families, students, and repair workers of every people. The [Ash Shadow](./kul-suikastcilari), called Ash Assassins by outsiders, appears where court, army, and shrine have together covered the wound.

The two orders are one another’s conscience. Knights say secret killing opens a new wound. Shadow cells answer that unpunished cruelty is already an open wound. Neither can wholly dismiss the other.

## Death, return, and account

Eshrenics do not call death sacred punishment. Honouring the dead and carrying the burden left to the living are part of the Debt of Repair. Resurrection is ethical only when the returning person consents, those awaiting them accept responsibility, and the return will not create new harm.

Forcing a dead person back for war, inheritance, or labour serves the Corrupting Will. The issue is not the name of death magic, but whether a person has been made into an instrument.

## Present tensions

Which harm enters the record? Who speaks a king’s lie? How far may punishment go when repair is impossible? And most difficult: does a person using violence to protect the world wound it further?

Eshrenic faith was formed against fear that evil will win. It never forgets that those who call themselves saviours can speak in corruption’s voice.
