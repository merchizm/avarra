# Marevi Bonds

<p class="lore-lead">For the Marevi, magic is not a source of power. Every spell enters a relation with something in the world: a place’s memory, ancestral Trace, a craft’s patience, a wind’s direction, or a spirit without a known name. If you do not know what you call, you do not know what listens.</p>

The tradition grew from dispersed spirit-medium practices once called the Nambari Spirit Houses, but it is no longer the religion of one people, pantheon, or medium family. **Marevi Bonds** is a shared root language of witch covens, local spirit-keepers, mediums, and theorists of magic. An Aškari quarter keeper, Sairavan healer, Eshrenic magic-restorer, or academic mage can draw on Marevi knowledge without becoming the other.

## What magic leaves behind

A Marevi knows magic is never only its result. A badly formed rite can injure a place’s Trace: the same nightmare repeating in well water, everyone in a house forgetting one name, unending anger on an old battlefield, or a spell a mage cast returning years later in their child’s dreams.

This does not blame every disaster on witches or spirits. Marevi practice rejects that shortcut. Disease may be bad water, bad air, or political neglect. But when magic has touched the wound, repairing body or stone is not enough; **the relation must be repaired too.**

Its ethic is simple: do not leave a debt upon the place, person, or spirit from which you took power. Ask what changed after the spell, who paid the cost, and whether the bond remains open.

## Witch covens

A witch coven is not three people in matching dress or shared blood. It is a bond too heavy for one person to hold, witnessed by at least three. One calls; one holds the boundary; one records cost and result. The roles can change in a night, but none may disappear.

This makes coven magic not only stronger but more accountable. If a witch performs a rite affecting a whole quarter alone, Marevi practice treats it with suspicion no matter how good the outcome looks. One person’s fear, pride, or grief can too easily enter everyone’s fate.

Witchcraft is not a blood right. A family may have held a bond with one spirit for generations, but the spirit owes the family nothing. If a household makes cruelty, a spirit may leave it. An adopted child, a neighbour of another people, or a stranger who has protected the land may make a truer bond than an old heir.

## Spirits and places

Marevi practice does not deny high gods, but does not assume they direct daily life. The spirits it approaches are not small, yet each is bounded and contextual.

- **Place spirits:** a well, bridge, abandoned house, pass, battlefield, old tree, or quarter.
- **Craft spirits:** the patience of a forge, guardian of a birth house, wakefulness of a caravan road.
- **Ancestral Traces:** not a true Name, but a family remainder made strong by long love, grief, and memory.
- **Carried spirits:** bonds brought across continent by migration, exile, and enslavement; they cannot live without negotiating with new place spirits.

This last distinction matters to diasporas. A Marevi community can carry an old spirit with it, but cannot set it above a new neighbourhood, river, or graveyard. A good bond-keeper does not say “our spirit rules here.” They first listen to a place’s memory, then seek a way for old and new bonds to speak without injury.

## Spirit mediumship and the Rule of Three Voices

For Marevi, a spirit approaching a medium’s body is not horrifying deviation. It can be regulated worship for divination, healing, search for the lost, or reconciliation. A body is never an open door, however. Without the **Rule of Three Voices**, mediumship is not legitimate:

1. The person offering the body gives explicit, revocable consent.
2. One coven member holds the boundary and remembers what the spirit may not say or do.
3. A witness outside the rite records the return, duration, and cost.

A spirit does not own a medium’s memories, speech, desires, or life. A thing that attempts this is not an invited guardian but a harmful bond. At the end, the medium’s own Name is spoken aloud and witnesses agree that no unwanted word is binding.

## Witches, mages, and pacts

Marevi Bonds does not claim every magic-user. Witches live within a bond; mages study its theory, names, and boundaries; druids keep the oldest common language with place; warlocks work within an explicit pact. These are not the same thing.

Academic schools have dismissed Marevi knowledge as a primitive precursor, then struggled to explain why great rites require place-names, witnesses, old objects, and boundary rules. Even the best arcane theorists concede that knowing a spirit’s name is not knowing how to speak with it.

Warlock pacts are not sin by default. Marevi ask whether both parties spoke freely, who carries the cost, and whether the bargain opens hidden wounds in other lives. Some bond-keepers seal trustworthy pacts; others risk their lives to unravel a king’s war pact.

## Dark bonds

Marevi practice opposes not the existence of spirits, but the use of people and spirits as tools.

**Empty Vessels** treat mediums as portable containers, using frightened or weakened people in long rites and selling the resulting voice as authority. **Crown Binders** chain a city guardian or ancestral Trace to a ruler’s will, trapping a shared memory in one crown. **Nameless Covens** discard coven witness and give every rite to one “high witch,” exploiting fear, blackmail, and the desire to learn magic.

The answer is not a witch hunt. Marevi first restore consent, make the bond’s cost visible, protect the medium’s Name, and help the local community rebuild its relation together. Anti-magic violence can open another wound.

## Tensions with other faiths

Theskarites accompany the dead through the Threshold; Marevi accept cautious relation with the Trace left behind. Both condemn the Whispering Crossroads’ exploitation of grief and dead people. Eshrenics say “repair harm”; Marevi add “repair the bond magic left behind.”

Vardûni jurists may call some mediumship false revelation or dangerous mediation. Sairavan readings dispute whether ancestral Trace is return or only a chain of consequence. In Aškari quarters, people can argue for generations over whether a city face and a quarter spirit are two names for the same presence.

## Present questions

Is a spirit truly speaking, or is the medium’s fear speaking? How long can a family bear an ancestral Trace? If a magical accident harms a whole quarter, who owes the bond’s repair? And above all: when does relating to a spirit become a claimed right to decide in its name?

Marevi Bonds offers no easy comfort. To form a relation is not to own it.
