# Star Order

<p class="lore-lead">The Star Order holds that divine intent can be read in the movement of stars, and that rulership and great decisions should be measured against celestial calculation. Stars do not issue commands; they show which doors time is opening and closing.</p>

## Calculation is not prophecy

Star readers chart eclipses, wandering lights, and seasonal turns. Births, coronations, departures, and major treaties receive sky tables. These tables do not announce a certain outcome. They describe the costs and possibilities a decision carries. A good reader treats a chart not as an instrument that decides for a ruler, but as a record to which the ruler must answer.

Temple observatories survive through night watches, measurement training, and open records. Sailors learn route-finding in their schools; jurists consult observation books to show that dates were not altered. The Order is therefore not only an aristocratic faith.

## Fixed meridian and wandering star

Fixed Meridian readers believe fate and dynastic order are largely set. At its best, this view says power too has heavenly limits; at its worst, it calls birth privilege a natural order. Wandering Star readers argue that signs offer a choice and leave its responsibility to mortals. Looking at the same chart, one may say “wait,” the other “prepare and choose.”

The Order’s ethical rule is that a bad result cannot be laid at the stars’ feet. If the sky gave warning, the one who heard it was expected to act with greater care.
