# House of Nine Fords

<p class="lore-lead">The House of Nine Fords preserves the oldest temple order of the Sairava basin. The world, it says, stands between nine great currents; no dynasty, birth, or flood lies outside their order.</p>

Its priests understand the Nine Currents as both powers and inevitable turnings of life: flood, birth, sickness, harvest, death, memory, war, mist, and return. Ford temples once administered irrigation shares, produce tax, and funeral calendars together. Their direct power is divided now, but the House remains formidable through lineage ledgers, water oaths, and dynastic rites.

## Doctrine: the Name finds its river

The House teaches that Breath disperses into the basin and Trace grows through action, but **Name** can echo again in houses and institutions that have carried it for generations. This does not simply mean “the same person returned.” Cautious priests call it an inclination, burden, or responsibility; court readers often turn that nuance into a claim for a throne.

Its most valued object is therefore not a sacred sword but the **Ford Ledger**: a record joining births, adoptions, oaths, funerals, and water shares across generations.

## Rites and political danger

First Water touches a newborn’s brow; Name Binding records adoption and household departure; at the Silent Ford, a dead person’s name is read nine times, softer on every reading. When a child is declared a returned ruler, ford-keepers demand three independent ledgers and the child’s freely given voice. Court priests call delay a danger to order. Rival dynasties call the child both sacred charge and political threat.

The House does not absolutely deny return across peoples, but demands grave proof before a dynastic Name can be judged to have crossed that boundary. It is attacked both by conservatives who want absolute exclusion and reformers who reject such boundaries altogether.
