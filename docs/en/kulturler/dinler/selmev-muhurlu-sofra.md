# Selmev’s Sealed Table

<p class="lore-lead">At Selmev’s first table there were bread, water, and an empty place. In later centuries the same table was surrounded by shrine estates, grain stores, and credit ledgers.</p>

The Sealed Table teaches that a community is responsible before God for one another’s hunger. Every great meal must make visible the share of debtor, widow, traveller, and dead. The principle also allowed powerful shrines to gather land, stores, and offerings; priestly houses grew wealthier than rulers in many cities.

Today’s faith divided around reform after that corruption. The Old Seals say a great table requires property and clergy to survive. The Open Bowl movement says the seal exists only to protect the poor and that shrine treasure is an offering held back.

For Selmevi law, food distribution is political. Whose stores open during siege, whose debt is released, and who the empty chair truly awaits are a city’s account before God.
