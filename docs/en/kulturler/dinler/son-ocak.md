# Last Hearth

<p class="lore-lead">Last Hearth teaches that a human spirit passes through the shared fire of family and people before returning to the Whole. It sees exile as more than loss of land: it is the scattering of a shared fire.</p>

## The carried ember

Every Last Hearth settlement keeps a small ember brought from another hearth and never allowed to die. Its record names not only the kingdom it came from, but also the hands that carried it. When a new neighbourhood is founded, its spark is joined with fuel brought by neighbours: the place may become home without denying the past.

At funerals, fire is not merely an instrument of burning but a witness that keeps a name warm. Giving fuel to a poor funeral is a public duty independent of family. In displacement, hearth keepers often become the faith’s practical institution by maintaining food, child care, and records of the missing.

## The shadow of lost crowns

Crown Keepers believe restored human kingdoms will make the fire whole again. Ash Children answer that a new society matters more than old dynasties, and that the ember’s strength lies in sharing. The Nameless pray for the dead of the Fifth Kingdom without pretending to know every lost name.

Last Hearth is therefore both tender and political. When a dynasty calls people back in the name of an old crown, adherents ask: to which hearth, with whom, and under what safety? Fire is sacred not because it returns the past unchanged, but because it makes warmth without leaving others outside.
