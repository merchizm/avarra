# Root Compact

<p class="lore-lead">The Root Compact teaches that old forests think through roots, fungi, dead, and memory. A grove is not timber or a line on a map, but a neighbour that remembers what has been entrusted to it.</p>

## Entrusted land

Grove and field cannot be sold under the Compact; they can only be used under a term of responsibility. A household that farms land agrees to protect the watercourse, grave, and shade-right left by those before it. Kinship may begin that right, but does not end it: a neighbour who has long tended the place, a migrant who carries seed, or a worker who restores land after fire can become a party to the Compact.

Root records are often kept through signs rather than writing. Knotted cords that do not injure bark, seed pouches left at stones, and seasonal walks indicate which ground must rest and for how long. When cutting is needed, the teaching favours a branch that can return over an old root that cannot.

## Open branch, closed root

Closed Root readers fear extraction by outsiders and seal sacred ground tightly. Open Branch insists that right follows reciprocal responsibility rather than blood. The Fading Circle calls Stagnation life’s true illness and argues that even old compacts must be renewed in new conditions.

These divisions are not abstract. Roads, refuge, hunting rights, and cutting are renegotiated in every council. The Compact never simply says “touch nothing.” It asks whether, after touching a place, you leave a future it can remember.
