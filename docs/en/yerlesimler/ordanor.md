# Ordanor

<p class="lore-lead">Ordanor is where the empire tells its most persuasive story: clean aqueducts, public schools, regular bread, working post, and an address language anyone can read. The same order continually asks who owns a home, a faith, a job, and a movement. The capital lives inside that contradiction; not just the palace, but hundreds of neighbourhoods, villages, and towers rebuild it every day.</p>

<div class="record-layout"><div><h2>City identity</h2><p><b>Function:</b> imperial capital, high bureaucracy, education, religious registration, money, and intercontinental government. <b>Population:</b> 10.8–13.6 million registered city residents; another 3.8–5.4 million people in the capital basin by season. <b>Peoples:</b> Veyran, human, Gnome, and Hobgoblin majorities; almost every people of the empire appears through a neighbourhood, guild, embassy, or temporary-work network. <b>Faith:</b> a plural system overseen by the Seal Order.</p><h2>City logic</h2><p>Ordanor is a plain city of two main meridians and seven intersecting rings. The meridians move the state quickly; the rings distribute neighbourhoods, markets, and water. Older canals, cellars, and unnamed streets run under the plan. The plan does not control everything, but strongly shapes who is visible, temporary, or called unregistered.</p><h2>Present fracture</h2><p>Representatives of three throne claimants hold separate wings of the same palace. Grain, tower watches, and post routes are supposedly neutral public service; in practice every appointment becomes a loyalty dispute. General Varessa’s support in the Training City, the Fifth Province’s citizenship demand, and people who do not fit the official record are wearing the capital’s order down from within.</p></div><aside class="record-profile"><figure class="city-concept-art"><img src="/assets/illustrations/cities/ordanor-concept.png" alt="" /></figure></aside></div>

Ordanor was not chosen because an empire already existed; the empire grew because it held this junction. Its controlled, level water plain binds grain convoys, training columns, and intercontinental messages to the same rings. The capital is only a centre while all four approaches remain open; closing one shows why its order needs so many towers and records.

## Entering the city

The distant traveller first sees aqueducts and the **North Measure Gate**, not the palace. No one must give a name at the gate, but cargo, animals, weapons, and intention to lodge overnight are recorded. Short-stay visitors receive a coloured clay tablet; a person settling in needs a neighbourhood witness or guild sponsor. On paper, the rule serves safety and bread distribution. People experience it very differently depending on whether they have the right witness.

Four great roads enter the city: the **Grain Meridian** from Halvek, the **Training Road** from Serath, the **Pilgrimage and Measure Road** from Red Meridian, and **Barge Avenue** from eastern river quays. At each route’s end, porters, travellers, and diplomats use the same *guest scale* courtyard. Weights, wages, and bed shares are posted there; delaying those postings causes neighbourhood pressure, not a minor administrative error.

### A day in Ordanor

At dawn, Meridian Tower sounds three short bells: water gates open, bread shares begin, and schools and offices open. In afternoon, neighbourhood announcers call out fires, missing people, road repairs, and public meetings. A second evening bell clears heavy carts from the main meridians; after a third, tower lamps guide traffic, though some areas require a neighbourhood witness for late passage.

The benefit is real: the notice network can find a lost child, water gates can contain a fire, and bread shares can move fast in scarcity. So is the cost: a person without address, work, or sponsor can disappear first in a queue, then in the register, and finally from aid. Most Ordanor reformers want not to destroy order, but to change who holds the witness and the record.

## Institutions that govern the capital

- **Council of Three Seals:** deputies signing for the throne claimants. None has an undisputed right to rule alone while the emperor is absent.
- **Ring Assemblies:** small elected bodies for water, bread, streets, and housing. They appear minor, but become the city’s real power during evacuation or quarantine.
- **High Register:** holds birth, death, property, school, and service records. Corrections take a long path, making free scribes and neighbourhood mediators vital.
- **Meridian Guard:** secures gates, bridges, and main roads. It works with tower watches, but a tower report alone is not grounds for arrest.
- **Open Bread Office:** manages granaries, bakeries, and emergency shares. It is most visible in hunger and therefore easiest to politicise.
- **Quiet Bureau:** shared rooms of translators, mapmakers, and letter decoders. Officially it secures communication; civic groups argue about the limits of that power.

## The capital basin: villages, quays, and production

Ordanor cannot feed itself. Villages one or two days beyond the walls belong to the capital basin, yet they are not just food stores: each has its own council, livelihood, and bargain with the palace.

| Place | Population / work | Link to the capital | Present issue |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Seven Mills** | 3,500–4,800; flour, waterwheels, baking coal | Delivers daily flour to the Open Bread Office | Falling river level turns mill order into a fight |
| **Column Plain** | 2,200–3,100; grain, pulses, horse feed | First granary stop on the Grain Meridian | A “temporary” military levy is consuming the harvest share |
| **Three Thresholds** | 1,600–2,400; inn, care house, road market | First witness and guest network for newcomers | Gate records are cutting guest income |
| **Lime Rift** | 1,000–1,700; stone, lime, brick, aqueduct repair | Tower and public-works builders live here | Quarry workers demand safe watches |
| **Reed Count** | 2,800–3,600; vegetables, reed, canal care | Fresh food and flood defence | A new canal line cuts three gardens |
| **Barge Rest** | 1,900–2,600; bargework, fish, storage | Final control point on the eastern river route | Lost cargo has made the whole village suspect |
| **Threshold Vine** | 900–1,300; grapes, medicinal herbs, funeral flowers | Supplies palace, hospitals, and Registry of Gods | The sacred garden is being claimed as public property |

These places pay capital taxes and receive road repair, schools, fire pumps, and grain insurance. On paper it is an equal exchange. In practice, villages know decisions are made in Ordanor, and “capital security” makes every bargain heavier.

## Watchtowers and road safety

The tower network is not one secret eye, but a chain of visible watch posts. Each gives weather, road, fire, and flood signals while reporting traffic and military movement. Watchers work in paired shifts, and a local civilian witness marks the daily ledger—an imperfect safeguard against the tower power passing wholly to the barracks.

| Tower | Place and public work | Dispute |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **First Meridian** | Palace ring: time, notices, main-route signal | Which claimant’s meeting bells first |
| **North Measure** | Grain-road gate: cargo scale and fire sign | Coloured-tablet registration for travellers |
| **Aqueduct Watch** | Great aqueduct junction: leak and sabotage warning | Water cuts are not equally shared |
| **Reed Lantern** | Reed Count canals: flood and fog beacon | Night patrol disturbs garden holders |
| **Seven Wheel** | Mill road: river level and bridge safety | The ledger deciding wheel order was altered |
| **Red Threshold** | Training Road: traveller quarantine and post relay | Military control closes a civilian route |
| **Barge Eye** | River quay: storm flag and rescue call | A lost-cargo notice becomes political embargo |

Towers use lamps, flags, bells, and short-range message magic. If all communication fails at once, the capital breaks into sections within an hour. Bakers, bargers, child message-runners, and neighbourhood announcers are therefore not backups to the official system; they are what lets it live.

## Factions and crisis clock

| Power | What it wants | What happens if it loses |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Varessa’s Training Circle** | Regular military appointments and fast decisions | Towers answer to rival commanders |
| **Odran’s Open Registrars** | Record correction, provincial representation, free trade | Bureaucracy and city trades split apart |
| **Seal Order hardliners** | Tighter faith and movement control | Registry of Gods becomes open opposition |
| **Fifth Province assemblies** | Witness right, tenant security, local representation | Evacuation and bread queues turn into street resistance |
| **Tower Watchers’ Union** | Neutral watches, wages, civic oversight | One tower incident collapses trust in the entire chain |

Crisis clock: delayed grain → reduced bread share → argument over protecting village convoys → politicised tower watches → “temporary” gate closures. The clock can be turned back, because at every stage a village council, ring assembly, or ordinary watcher can decide otherwise.

## Adventure starts

- Seven Wheel Tower’s river ledger has been rewritten to show a night with no water loss.
- A family suspended from the register at Three Thresholds may be hiding the only witness who carried imperial post.
- One capital tower rings an evacuation bell before any official decision; everyone debates whether it is error or warning.
- If Threshold Vine’s flower share is taken for a palace funeral, hospitals and neighbourhood rites will not have enough.
- A child from the Unregistered knows every daytime tower password. No one wants to explain how.
