# Free City of Winter Granaries

<p class="lore-lead">The tallest structures in Winter Granaries are ventilation towers, not palaces. One closed storehouse door can leave an army hungry hundreds of miles away months later.</p>

<div class="record-layout"><div><h2>City identity</h2><p><b>Function:</b> free city of war supply, grain credit, and convoy contracts. <b>Population:</b> 620–920k permanent residents; approaches 1.2m in harvest and campaign season. <b>Peoples:</b> Humans, Halflings, Hobgoblins, Dwarves, Duergar, and Warforged are concentrated here; smaller communities arriving by river have also made permanent quarters. <b>Government:</b> mutual oversight among the Provision Senate, carrier guilds, and storehouse guards.</p><h2>City logic</h2><p>The city grew around high, dry stores. River branches from the grain plain enter barge basins; the basins open onto paved convoy roads and then to gated outer granaries. No cargo is piled in one place: fire, flood, or a raid cannot take the whole winter at once.</p><h2>Present fracture</h2><p>Two debtor crowns are queued for the same fodder and flour allocation. If the Senate preserves a neutral timetable, both armies will be delayed; if it gives one priority, the city must risk the other’s siege.</p></div><aside class="record-profile"><figure class="city-concept-art"><img src="/assets/flags/kis-ambarlari.png" alt="Free City of Winter Granaries flag" /></figure></aside></div>

## From grain to campaign

**High Granaries** are the oldest district on the city’s stone ridges. Air channels, rat traps, fire walls, and counting rooms matter more here than display architecture. A granary master may speak to more people than a crown’s envoy, because they often have the first say in which sack is set aside for whom.

**Barge Basins** draw river water into the city. Grain, salt, oil, flax, timber, and metal parts pass here from small boats to cranes, then from cranes to caravan carts. Loading schedules are not public—not only for fear of spies, but to conceal which reserve route will open when another fails.

**Convoy Gates** remind people that more than goods leave the city. Their guards are armed, but trained to protect stores, bridges, and carriers rather than conquer. Hobgoblin veterans stand gate watches, Halfling family networks organise distribution, Dwarf and Duergar masters repair, and Warforged citizens handle heavy counting and movement. None are a people inherently tied to one work; duties are shared through guild and civic law, not inheritance.

## The unseen economy

In Winter Granaries, credit lives in a daily calendar rather than a decorated vault. Firewood for a bakery, arrival of animal feed, a replacement part for a mill, and the lock on a frontier store may all be parts of one contract. The city does something quieter than impoverishing a ruler who fails to pay: it moves that ruler’s shipments down the queue.

Quarter life is not outside this system. During convoy season courtyards become temporary dormitories; children learn cargo marks, elders carry news along routes, and small shops close to shift hours. The city grew by learning that supplying an army also means keeping its own kitchens and wells alive.
