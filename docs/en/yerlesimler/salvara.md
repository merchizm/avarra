# Sâlvara

<p class="lore-lead">In Sâlvara’s harbour, ships carry sealed hourglasses as readily as spice, iron, or grain. On the terraces above the shore stand the gardens and year vaults of the wealthy; below them, captive ships, fading clinics, and court queues face one another.</p>

## Above the vault, below the quay

**Seven Vaults** are the upper terraces of Hourglass Council houses, including cellars, measuring rooms, and transfer halls. **Fading Quay** receives captive ships, debtors, and care carts. **Sand Market** trades small year-shares, transfer promises, counterfeit seals, and care medicines. **Late Gardens** lie between sea-facing mansions and quiet terraces for elderly citizens; their vaults keep the price of the city’s promise that old age can be defeated.

## City culture

Sâlvarans speak of time as wealth that can be stored, divided, and inherited. In the workers’ districts, another custom persists: on a child’s birthday, a small lock of hair is kept outside the family record. This **Remaining Share** is a fragile personal witness that the person’s years have not been sold. Healers, carers, priests, escape networks, and citizens who have lost their own years all oppose harvest law in different ways, though the vaults often entangle even their resistance in debt and care dependency.

## Table-ready starts

- A sealed year-share sold in Sand Market matches the name of a prisoner who can still speak.
- A Late Gardens vault opens. The years do not return, but everyone in the house that took them ages in a single night.
- A healer on Fading Quay finds a forbidden method that can save a prisoner’s organs without taking their years.
