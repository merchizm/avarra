Forty Bridges was built not upon stone but upon right of passage. Each island has its own granary, night bell, and bridge law; closing one bridge changes the balance not of two districts but of the entire river trade.

## Forty small republics

The Founding Compact treats the bridges not only as engineering works but as forty equal partners. **Poplar Bridge** controls grain barges, **Copper Arch** craft freight, **Seven Ropes** passenger passage, and **Reed Foot** access for fishers and marsh peoples. The council seats one master, one carrier, and one shore witness for each bridge, so even a small island can stop a great guild.

The debate over a forty-first bridge strikes at that law. Its night-by-night construction and morning demolition may be more than sabotage: elder builders claim the river is cutting a new branch and that the compact was written on a current, not on stone.

## Table tensions

- A bridge guild has replaced missing members with puppet witnesses and quietly bought the council.
- The forty-first bridge remains intact at dawn; the city splits over whose law governs its first traveller.
- Floodwater is about to take Reed Foot, and preserving it requires deliberately closing the other bridges.
