At Sazkalp, a boundary is drawn not by a wall but by which channel receives water. Fixed islands hold archives, granaries, and the old dynastic structure; living platforms move to good water. That tension makes the capital resilient and renegotiated every year.

## City of the canal council

**Three Weirs** is the council island that votes the year’s water direction. **Root Market** brings together reed boats, rice, and medicinal-plant cargoes; **Floating Gardens** represent the villages’ grain and seed rights. Water engineers do not merely dig ditches: they decide which village plants, which district gathers disease, and who can reach the capital by boat.

This year the vote to change a channel is unexpectedly tied. A small margin of error can enrich one village and starve another, making embankment masters, priests, and boat families the kingdom’s most powerful politicians.

## Table tensions

- Reed roots begin showing an old channel direction by themselves on the night of the vote.
- One Floating Garden disappears with an entire village’s seed right aboard.
- Both rival engineers claim the water measure at Three Weirs as sacred.
