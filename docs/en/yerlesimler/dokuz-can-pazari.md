The “centre” of Nine Souls Market moves with every monsoon. The market settles on whichever of nine platforms survives the water best, and the record guilds must move their contracts too. That mobility preserves knowledge while making ownership of every word a continuing dispute.

## A market of information

**Living Ledgers** is the level where Kenku record guilds keep spoken, imitated, and witnessed contracts. **Nine Boughs** are hanging ways where Tabaxi story families trade narrative, lineage, and passage rights. **Rain Bowl** is the river square where debts of information are paid in water, silk, and medicine. A contract is valid only when both its record is heard and its story recognised.

Information is property here, but not merely a thing sold: recording someone’s memory wrongly can change their debt or kinship. Kenku guilds say no word exists legally without a record; Tabaxi families say a record without story is not a living person.

## Table tensions

- Two living records of the same contract prove both sides right.
- One of Nine Boughs breaks in a monsoon night, yet every story standing on it moves to another bough.
- A childhood memory sold at Rain Bowl is demanded back as the price of powerful healing.
