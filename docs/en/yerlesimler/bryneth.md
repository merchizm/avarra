# Bryneth

<p class="lore-lead">Bryneth is an old Wood Elf city that lived among forest clearings before and after the War of Roses. It now stands within the Thorn Crown’s law, yet its districts keep the names of old states not as dynastic property but as the memory of lost groves.</p>

The city accepts no single account of the old war. Some call Bryneth the wounded heart of a defeated state; others a meeting place for families that changed sides. What is certain is that thousands of years did not leave it a ruin: old defensive ditches became gardens, watchtowers became bird houses, and war roads became market roads.

## City life

**Open Branch Square** holds root law, trade, and district decisions; **White Bark** contains old tree-houses, scribes, and memory gardens; **Understream** carries mills, dye works, and small crafts. Wood Elves are the largest people, while Firbolg, Forest Gnome, human, Satyr, and other forest families are citizens as well.

The current tension is how the past should be remembered. Families loyal to the Thorn Crown say war names only feed new division. Local memory keepers answer that erasing names is the victor’s final victory.

## Villages of the Bryneth belt

| Settlement | Scale and function | Current tension |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eirwen** | 1,800–2,600 people; nurseries, medicinal plants, grafted trees | Opposes distributing rare seeds only with Crown Council approval. |
| **Silver Stream** | 1,100–1,700 people; mill, bridge, river transport | Is opening old supply tunnels trade, or disrespect for war graves? |
| **Dewfield** | 900–1,400 people; grain and pulses in a forest clearing | A new Thorn Crown use term shortens the farming calendar. |
| **Whitethorn** | 700–1,100 people; beekeeping, wax, thorn-fence maintenance | Root Guardians claim the fences are becoming military works. |
| **Leaflake** | 800–1,300 people; reeds, fishing, funeral boats | Debate continues over recovering old banners from the lake bed. |
| **Silent Branch** | 400–700 people; enclosed grove, fruit, small crafts | No one knows what the families resisting outside records conceal. |

These villages are not Bryneth’s property. Each is tied to the city through market, road, and grove agreements, and defends its own use rights against the Thorn Crown.

## SD 83: names beneath the lake

Low water at Leaflake reveals a stone floor bearing three old city seals. Bryneth’s memory keepers want it protected; a merchant guild argues that the pre-war road can be reopened. That same week, seeds said to belong to groves marked by the seals sprout in Eirwen.
