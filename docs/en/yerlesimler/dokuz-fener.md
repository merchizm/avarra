Nine Lanterns maps not area but light, water, air, and collapse time. Knowing the shortest tunnel is not enough to reach First Light: without the right lantern’s shift sound, a traveller can walk the same path for hours.

## A confederation of nine cities

Nine Lanterns is not one city. First Light is its political centre, not the capital of the other eight. Each city keeps its own law, guard, lantern, and daily life. They are bound not by a common ancestry or ruler, but by knowing that being alone underground is fatal.

To be Nimrûn is to be bound to Nine Lantern law, citizenship beneath a city lantern, silence on common roads, and Union defence. Rock Gnomes, Kobolds, and Warforged can be citizens on the same terms. Nimrûn power is not looking large; it is controlling what an enemy sees, hears, and mistakes for true.

## Lantern, law, and government

The Lantern at each city’s centre is a great crystal or bioluminescent structure. It holds public time, tunnel direction, emergency signals, vibration sense, the sound network, magical identity, and the civic oath. Pale white means normal life, slow blue rest, amber market or shift, red external attack, purple magical breach, green water or garden alarm, full darkness concealment, and reverse shadow foreign interference. Darkness does not mean a city has died; the wrong colour is the greater fear.

The Founding Compact says no lantern may own another, no common road may become a weapon, water and air may not be cut off in war, an attacked city must be aided, and a city is not fallen simply because its lantern goes dark. Only its own representative or the unanimous vote of the other eight may declare it fallen. That is why Last Glow’s seat cannot be removed.

Each city holds one vote in the Union Council. A Lantern Voice is not a king or governor but carries the city’s common decision; voting personal opinion is a grave crime. The council handles common defence, roads, foreign treaties, water-air-lantern networks, membership, evacuation, and war or peace. Tax, family, property, education, and local crime remain city matters. Major decisions require six votes. Because of Last Glow, the Eightfold Procedure accepts six living votes while the Ninefold Oath says no fundamental decision can be made without the ninth voice.

## Architecture and daily life

The cities use vertical streets, bridges, passages inside walls, spiral stairs, light wells, quiet courts, ceiling gardens, and water channels rather than level squares. Larger peoples stoop through low doors while Nimrûn use second and third streets in wall and ceiling voids. Crystal, fine mirror, and fungus screens cast guiding shadow instead of harsh light. Curved walls hide a home’s sound from the street, a workshop from a dwelling, and a true route from its echo.

Time follows lantern cycles. Cities keep close but not identical rhythms for trade. On a common road, people do not speak a real name, shout, call into an unknown void, sing without permission, or strike metal needlessly. A Hearth Name belongs to intimates; an Echo Name belongs to trade, politics, and strangers. Homes contain a vibration cord, warm room, sleeping niche, fungus shelf, water store, and hidden escape void. Children learn to read walls, live echoes, changes of air, false lanterns, collapse, and how to lead a follower down the wrong route.

First Echo listens for stone’s first sound; its priests tend tunnels, acoustic balance, old records, and funeral echoes. Silent Gem holds that unmined ore is future wealth and that some crystals must remain in place for lantern and water balance. Crystal records at Echo Well are evidence equal to a contract or seal, and the Right to Remain Silent prevents anyone being forced into an echo that incriminates them. Giving a common road to an enemy, imitating a lantern signal, harming air or water, concealing a collapse warning, changing a vote record, archiving real names, or opening a sacred vein are grave crimes.

## Communication and defence

Echo Lines carry short rhythms through crystal and metal for attacks, roads, water, council business, missing caravans, and lantern faults; long secrets travel by courier. Shadow Meter couriers never know an entire route. Defence aims not to win open battle but to prevent an enemy finding a target: a tunnel returns to its start, sound arrives backward, the road narrows or opens onto water, a false lantern draws troops into a trap gallery, or an outer ceiling falls under control. Broken Lantern uses vibration, imitation commands, and low frequencies. Small units cut routes, darken lights, sabotage supplies, make maps meaningless, and disappear.

First Light keeps nine separate vote rooms at the neutral junction, including a clean, lit empty chamber for Last Glow. Deep Lens exchanges knowledge with Astereth but fears theft of discoveries. Altering old records at Echo Well threatens authority. Blue Stone manages the other cities’ water reserve and feels the slowing at Namaris. Broken Lantern treats its crack as a survival symbol and seeks extraordinary power. Thin Bell is cosmopolitan, accused by traditionalists of becoming surface-like. Sleeping Ore faces pressure to open a sacred vein. Shadow Meter says an incomplete map is security. Last Glow’s light can be seen, but walking toward it does not bring it closer.

Three Currents distributes water neutrally; Root Quay transfers surface goods; Reverse Lantern is a false-city defence; Quiet Halt is a neutral traveller station; and Bottomless Threshold is the sealed outpost toward Last Glow. Warforged claim inheritance because they protected cities and lanterns while carrying the oaths of lost families.

## The vote of Last Glow

A sealed Echo Stone found in the empty voting niche bears Last Glow’s seal and the vibration of its former Lantern Voice. It seems to have arrived by a route absent from Shadow Meter records and contains a new voting date. Accepting it lets an unknown sender from an unreachable city influence the council. Rejecting it means silencing the ninth voice against the Union’s founding principle. Broken Lantern wants military power, Thin Bell wants routes opened, Shadow Meter wants links sealed, and Blue Stone wants the water crisis addressed first. The Union must decide whether keeping an empty place for a silent city preserves it, or whether that emptiness will one day consume it.
