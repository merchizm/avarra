# Varkesh

<p class="lore-lead">Varkesh descends from the plateau of a quarried mountain to two harbours. Above, narrow lanes hold the doors of war credit and public record; below, the shores learn whether those credits become grain, ships, and pay.</p>

## City from top to water

**Upper City** is not only where the wealthy live. Council courts, bathhouses, law houses, small workshops, and tenant homes pressed into old gardens share the plateau. Tile roofs, stone arches, iron balconies, and shaded passages make it look like a coastal court; behind its stairs people argue about grain price, tax, and soldiers leaving home.

**Treasury Steps** are the slope of credit courts, cisterns, scribal houses, and storehouses between plateau and harbour. An entire ship’s cargo may change hands here, but the room writing a note is not the building locking its goods. The separation is deliberate, making it harder for one family to seize a credit invisibly.

**Quietwater** is commerce’s calm face. Shallows break the sea for heavily laden vessels. Fishers and grain barges share the water; Simic maintenance crews check underwater piles, cargo chains, and the harbour mouth at night.

**Windgate** is less a quarter than a military threshold. Its towers watch open sea, its barracks hold the coast road, and its dry basins keep the fleet. Banner Command fears more than invasion: that a garrison openly fighting the Treasury Council could take Varkesh hostage in the name of protecting it.

## Daily life and unseen work

On market days Varkesh trades more than spice or cloth. Repair of a quarter cistern, unpaid wages of a lost sailor, salvage share from a wreck, and winter fuel for a soldier’s family can all be bargains. Kithkin courtyards run shared kitchens; Kender lanes move small cargo carts; Gargoyle slope homes hold night tailors and stoneworkers; Khoravar inns employ translators; Vedalken record rooms train apprentices. All are the unseen infrastructure of the two-key order.

The city likes its military and financial power to look orderly. But when a convoy is late, great vaults are not the first things to fail: quarter kitchens and harbour shifts are. A good ruler is therefore measured not by how much credit they open, but which cistern, bakery, and watch remains open when war is delayed.
