## The planned city

Astereth rises through nine terraces aligned to the Nine Stations of the Celestial Crown. Its approach never shows the palace directly: gardens, canals, academy towers, observatories, and finally Crown Star unfold in a calculated sequence. The trees are chosen to flower in different months so every visitor arrives through bloom. At the Pale Arch, invisible warding reads powerful magic, curses, bound spirits, and unauthorised passage marks. A guest receives a discreet silver Guest Star that records permitted districts, invitations, arms, magic, and length of stay.

Streets follow the rising and setting lines of stars. A new building needs architectural, astrological, magical, acoustic, and social-harmony reports; even a window angle may be planned. White starstone disperses heat by day, gives back stored light at night, carries magical lines, resists grime, and changes colour before it cracks. Its expense means that many lower-terrace white façades hide ordinary brick and timber.

## Star Network and the hidden city

The Star Network of silver, crystal, and worked stone powers lights, canal gates, observatory mirrors, fire signals, defensive seals, public clocks, and official message towers. It gathers celestial energy, stores it in crystal chambers, and reveals the approximate district of unlicensed major magic. Light is kept low so that the night sky remains sacred. Wind corridors, canals, shade trees, starstone, small weather seals, and seasonal veil magic civilise rather than erase weather.

The city’s spotless appearance also rests on thousands of night-shift workers. Organic waste becomes compost, alchemical material, feed, or fuel; dirty water crosses the Nine Channels’ filters, reed pools, and low-level cleansing magic. Astereth has not eliminated its flaws; it has often moved them out of the elite field of view.

## Districts, learning, and class

**Crown Star** holds Serel’s palace, throne, regency wing, observatory, map vault, silent garden, mirror reception hall, and dynasty graves. The throne turns with celestial positions; a war envoy and a marriage envoy enter through different doors. Mirrors identify dust, wrong colours, crooked badges, and diplomatic insults before a meeting. Serel’s day is divided among celestial law, languages, diplomacy, history, magic discipline, governance, ceremony, and rest, while rival households compete to choose his tutors.

**Meridian** joins star priests, observatories, calendar courts, and celestial archives. The Great Observatory calculates seasons, tides, Fey passages, world-vein movements, magical storms, planting, sailing, rites, and royal birth charts. Calendar Courts decide whether weddings, treaties, coronations, oaths, expeditions, or Fey pacts occurred at an appropriate celestial hour—an expensive privilege for lower classes to wait for.

**Silver Gardens** are planned emotions as much as planned plants, and house elite art, philosophy, magic, and craft. The academies of Celestial Magic, Form and Proportion, Elegant Defences, Living Light, Diplomatic Languages, Memory and Dream, Gnome Mechanics, and Healing Geometry require talent but also patronage, money, recommendations, and evaluated behaviour. **Nine Channels** is the Gnome engineering district: drinking, garden, bath, craft, fire, cooling, waste, observatory, and emergency water each have distinct routes. Gnomes built the city’s hidden motor but are still often treated as valuable specialists rather than equal founders.

**Overshadowed** is the lower-terrace world of servants, gardeners, cleaners, carriers, junior clerks, and migrants: crowded housing, damp service routes, weak light, and worse water. **False Star** is the irregular permanent district of foreign students, workers, Eladrin, Tieflings, half-elves, exiles, and expelled scholars. **Silent Children** is the closed institution where Aasimar children with uncontrolled light, prophetic dreams, spiritual calls, or involuntary healing are trained and watched; its protection is real, but family authority and abuse allegations remain uncertain.

## Magic law, defence, and the new star

Simple light and cleaning magic are free; healing is registered, illusion is limited in trade and identity, mind magic needs special permission, teleportation is confined to designated rooms, divination is supervised by the Star Order, summoning requires academy/state approval, necromancy is heavily regulated, climate and water magic need infrastructure permits, and time magic is prohibited except by royal authority. Cosmetic illusion cannot hide a commercial flaw or change identity in court. Unplanned windows, walls, or balconies can be serious crimes because they may interrupt observation, water, the network, or another home’s light.

The Celestial Veil detects major threats, Star Mirrors can become weapons, canal gates isolate terraces, Silent Guards protect palace and academies, and the network channels unauthorised teleportation toward reception rooms. Astereth is strong against magical attack but fragile under long siege because it depends on foreign grain, metal, timber, and continual maintenance.

House **Aelor** seeks to protect Serel and treat the new star as danger; **Miravel** wants research, a Frost Palace marriage, and stronger foreign alliances; **Selent** brings Gnome engineers, traders, water reform, and technical representation. The new star is not merely astronomical. If children of False Star saw it for years while official observatories did not, Astereth must ask whether its perfect order sees only the things for which it has chosen to make room.
