## Two cities overlaid

Halvek is one city from afar and two worlds within. The old human city grew around river bends, slopes, and century-old neighbourhood ties; its lanes are narrow and named through memory. After conquest, Ordan laid the **Meridian Plan** over it: straight ceremonial avenues, standard street widths, numbered blocks, measured boundaries, record towers, granaries, barracks, aqueducts, sewers, and troop squares. Roads sometimes cut houses in two: a kitchen may stand in the Fifth Registry District while the bedroom is taxed in the Sixth.

The vast Imperial Meridian runs north to south, wide enough for four chariots. The prefecture palace, Great Registry, provincial court, garrison headquarters, Nine Granaries, mint, Court of Measures, Celestial Crown temple, and victory columns line it. Brass lines every hundred Ordan paces define all city distances. Unlicensed vending, tethering animals, street shows, and funeral processions are forbidden: the avenue is a display of the state.

### Entry and identity

The busiest of seven gates is Counting Gate. Entrants declare name, origin, goods, arms, lodging district, and undergo health or magic inspection if required. Veyran clerks use wax tablets, coloured cords, and sealed paper.

| Tablet | Status |
| --- | --- |
| Brass-edged | Imperial official |
| Blue-edged | Registered citizen |
| Green-edged | Guild member |
| White-edged | Foreign trader |
| Red-striped | Soldier or armed officer |
| Grey | Temporary worker or refugee |
| Unedged clay | Person under observation |

Someone without a tablet is not automatically criminal, but cannot find work, lodge at an inn, buy at an official market, and risks detention at night. Halvek often punishes people not by prison, but by removing them from records.

## Districts and everyday life

**Administration Terraces** hold the prefecture, registries, embassies, and high officials in identical white-grey buildings. Doors bear offices, not names; children train early for writing, calculation, maps, formal speech, and examinations. **Old Stones** preserves guild houses, family shrines, and the old human centre. Its royal hall is now the Provincial Tax Appeals Court—order’s triumph to Veyrans, deliberate humiliation to humans.

**Broken Square** contains ruined monuments and unidentified mass graves; its unfinished victory column stopped when human bones were found. It is a cheap daytime market and a nighttime vigil for the nameless dead. **Counting Gate** combines inns, caravan yards, translators, identity writers, and seal forgers; birth records, dead identities, guild papers, travel permits, exemptions, and pedigrees can all be bought if the forgery matches every office’s records.

**Two Irises** is the main human–Veyran mixed district, with bilingual schools, mixed-marriage courts, blended home rites, and the food/music that is forming Halvek’s new identity. **Ashbelow** is built over old canals, cellars, and closed graves: poor workers, unregistered migrants, hidden families, illicit water, unlicensed shrines, clinics, shelters, and unions all exist here. Its cells include independence fighters, Broken Tower autonomists, labour groups, anti-war Veyrans, Red Stag militants, and ordinary smugglers.

**Bell Market** is the common market, governed by bells for opening, measure inspection, fire, and military closure. Scales are inspected weekly and broken in public if false. **Nine Granaries** is a walled storage district: no official can open all nine. Grain determines marching distance, bread prices, and which province can rebel; it, more than the prefecture, is the prize of succession. **River Barracks** joins docks, shipyards, warehouses, and barracks into a second military city of soldiers, rowers, porters, shipwrights, Hobgoblin auxiliaries, and Halfling barge families.

Three measuring sounds from Meridian towers open the gates, begin official work, and open market/granary records. Registered poor households receive grain or durable state bread by tablet; this eases hunger but makes suspended registration immediately a loss of food. Aqueducts, fountains, fire pools, and sewers give the empire real legitimacy, though water reaches Terraces, barracks, and state workshops before Ashbelow and Old Stones in drought. Schools promise merit through exams, but language, books, and free study time advantage clerk families. Night closes with a long bell; movement on the Meridian, near granaries, and in military districts requires a pass.

Halvek food joins imperial provisions and old human cooking: garlic grain soup, river fish with sour herbs, Veyran-spiced lentils, onion soldier bread, stuffed leaves, fried mushrooms, salted cheese, apple with dark beer, small pastry parcels, and honey-poppy festival buns. Standard bowl sizes are enforced; shrinking one is a crime against imperial measure.

## Records, faith, and army

The nine principal registers cover birth/death, household/residence, property/use, guild/profession, tax, military service, travel/migration, religious institutions, and magic. Mismatched entries trigger an investigation. Corruption commonly means changing a queue, finding the wrong file, or sending a record for “further review.” The feared building is the Great Registry Correction Office, where a lineage, marriage, property, or citizenship can vanish by one pen stroke.

The Celestial Crown presents imperial order as cosmic order. The Seal Order is its stricter Ordan reading, making law, measure, and record sacred; High Priest Maelor’s supporters seek to close unregistered faiths and strengthen Veyran governing status. The Last Hearth remains legal but watched in human districts. Small cults must register site, priests, income, magic, and annual rites; unregistered worship is treated as evasion of public order.

Spikewheat Fort weighs and seals rural tax grain; Varessa’s men seek it under a temporary war levy. Three Wharves backs Odran’s trade settlement; Papermoor can halt courts, taxes, and troop movement if its workers stop. Bellfield is celebrated as model farming, but city Veyran clerks own most land. Meridianhead receives imperial orders before Halvek, Ironnotch supplies the empire through standardised interchangeable parts, Old Garden hosts Broken Tower meetings, and Ninth Mile is Varessa’s base although its youth increasingly call themselves Halvekian.

The **Prefecture** tries to maintain order while offices leak information to their chosen claimant. **Human Guilds** oppose both the old nobles’ return and collapse of law, water, and trade; they demand representation and equal citizenship. **Broken Tower** seeks an autonomous human province within Ordan and may work with Odran. **Red Stag** sees civil war as the opening for independence and prepares attacks on the garrison, granaries, and Meridian symbols. **Ashbelow Leagues** unite labour rights, record reform, refugee protection, and anti-war politics. The three claimants’ “succession liaison offices” distribute money, buy officials, recruit soldiers, and spread propaganda.
