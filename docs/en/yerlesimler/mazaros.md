Mazaros’s harbour is not one bay but seven seas that conceal one another. It can protect great fleets—and lose them: a bad guide can take a warship not into a shipyard but into a sealed cave or shoal. The demand for a common fleet is therefore a proposal to place every key to this labyrinth in one council.

## One of the Four Labyrinth Ports

Mazaros is not a capital. The four Labyrinth Ports are not one state but four sovereign republics, and Mazaros is one of them. Its population, shipyards, and war fleet make outsiders treat it as the de facto centre, which troubles the other three republics.

## The Seven Inner Seas

Where an old mountain belt sank beneath the sea, the limestone coast formed nested coves, arches, straits, caves, sinkhole harbours, underwater passages, and cliff terraces. A cave system collapsed and filled millennia ago, producing today’s seven basins. Mazaros is therefore not a planned metropolis but the union of seven old port towns specialising in fishing, trade, warships, repair, salt, foreign traffic, and storm refuge. Without a local guide, a fleet cannot tell which channel leads to a shipyard, a shoal, or a sealed cave.

**Great Horn** is the main trade and customs port. **Iron Bay** is the chain-gated military yard and rapid-repair centre. **Hidden Water** shelters storm traffic and reserve fleets. **Salt Bowl** handles salt, fish, leather, and the Tiefling Salt Horn district. **Quiet Yard** builds diplomatic and magical cave ships. **Foreign Water** holds embassies, inns, insurance, and translators. **Deep Mouth** is the deep basin for Triton, Sea Elf, Water Genasi, and underwater trade.

Shipyards, fish markets, warehouses, and heavy work sit at sea level; homes, taverns, rope and sail workshops, and courts on the middle terraces; captain families, the council, lighthouse, observatory, towers, and cisterns high on the cliffs. Salt-worn stone, basalt, painted plaster, wooden balconies, copper roofs, sails, and the sounds and smells of wave, hammer, rope, tar, salt, fish, and spice make the city. Astereth impresses; Mazaros swallows.

## Labyrinth law and daily life

For Minotaurs, the labyrinth is not a trap but a sacred sign that there is no single correct way. In serious matters, people examine the shortest, safest, and most reversible routes; indirect speech by diplomats does not hide truth but carries its consequences. Addresses are stories of return: “where three captains returned, on the road facing land rather than sea.”

The Shipyard Forest holds thousands of masts, cranes, and keels with a multi-people work hearth. Salt Horn’s Tiefling ancestral houses sustain supplies through work, health, law, funeral, and child care. Wind Steps connects cliffs; Old Turn preserves ancient law and trains judges; Four Rudders tests captains and maze skill; Forty Ropes is the sailors’ people; High Horn is the council and temple terrace. Road Courts hear cases by walking them through Old Turn and debating alternative outcomes at every stop. Citizenship requires not ancestry but harbour or hearth membership, sea-safety and law examinations, and an oath to leave a return route.

Daily life begins with tide and shipyard-shift tones. People travel by cliff path, inner-harbour boat, and cave canal. Rain and springs are gathered in cisterns; damaging them can be treason. Grilled fish, octopus, salted meat, shellfish, chickpeas, olives, sour bread, cheese, pepper soup, dates, figs, salted root vegetables, and Tiefling baked dishes make the common table, where routes, trade, and law are discussed.

## Economy, defence, and the common fleet

Shipbuilding and repair, convoy work, maps and guiding, fishing, salt, rope and sail, law, sailor training, salvage, and mercenary fleets sustain the city. Timber, iron, hemp or flax, grain, tar, and water vessels must be imported. Narrow entries, chain gates, false lanterns, cave passages, cliff ballistae and magical mirrors, and basins that can be drained form its defence. Taking Mazaros requires control not of an outer gate but of each Inner Sea.

Mazaros calls a common fleet necessary against pirates, Ordan expansion, sea monsters, and Saar competition. Vel-Corridor wants financiers to have the final word, Horn Harbour wants mercenary captains independent, and Turngate wants ancient law protected from military order. The danger is not Mazaros aggression: it is that a unity genuinely needed for survival can become Mazaros dominion over time.
