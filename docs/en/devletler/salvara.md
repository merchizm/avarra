# Sâlvara

<p class="lore-lead">Sâlvara is the slaveholding state and port city of the same name on the northwestern shore of Slave Bay. Here wealth is measured not in gold, but in stored years: people ask not how many houses a family owns, but how many human lifetimes sit in its vaults.</p>

Sâlvara’s wealth comes from the magic called **lifespan harvesting**. Years of life taken from prisoners of war, convicts, and enslaved people are held in magical vessels; the state weighs, seals, taxes, and transfers those shares to foreign buyers. The taken years steal not only a person’s future, but also their present: the body ages rapidly, organs weaken, and most victims soon require intensive care.

The state calls this not slavery, but punishment, restitution, and the “productive circulation of lifespan.” Its law draws a hard line between a free citizen and a person who may be harvested, though war, debt, and foreign treaties widen that line every year.

## Wealth in years

Sâlvara’s basic unit of trade is the **year-share**: a span of life taken from a reliable source and sealed for use. A year can be transferred into a buyer’s body, not to make them young, but to delay approaching age and organ failure. Every transfer loses something. The most valuable commodity is therefore a freshly sealed year violently taken from a healthy young body.

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| **Lifespan harvesting** | A forbidden magical practice that separates years of life from the body. |
| **Year-share** | A stored, measured, transferable unit of lifespan. |
| **Sand vault** | A state-controlled storehouse where year-shares rest in sealed glass, crystal, or hourglasses. |
| **Fading debt** | The obligation to pay debt from one’s own years or a family’s future labour. |
| **Final commission** | The state’s share of a harvest performed for a foreign crown. |

A year-share is not money, but it can secure money, land, or military aid. A dynasty can borrow from Sâlvara by pledging not future taxes, but the three hundred year-shares needed by its ageing ruler. After wars, the state therefore buys not only captives but also the succession calendars of defeated courts.

## The Hourglass Council

The **Hourglass Council** rules Sâlvara. Seats gain weight not through lineage, but through the quantity of year-shares entrusted to state vaults. This keeps wealthy families in power for generations, though their members are not always young. A councillor who has sold or lost their own years may look visibly old while holding thousands in reserve.

**Harvest Courts** decide which years may be taken from criminals, debtors, and prisoners of war. **Vault Wardens** protect stores and transfer rites. **Measurers** test the purity of years. These institutions appear to restrain one another, but all share the same premise: with sufficient judgement and seals, one person’s lifespan can become another’s wealth.

## Coast, neighbours, and foreign trade

Sâlvara lies northwest of [Umbraval](./umbraval-serbest-sehri), on the shore of Slave Bay. Umbraval registers the right to use shadows; Sâlvara harvests lifespan. Neither state is well placed to condemn the other, yet competition over harbour traffic, captives, and contracts frequently sets them against each other.

Foreign kingdoms can transfer prisoners of war, serious convicts, or debtor populations to Sâlvara. In return they receive year-shares, long-term credit, transfer rights for ageing rulers, or military supplies. In every agreement Sâlvara demands a harvesting, storage, and transfer commission. Its neutrality concerns neither who provides the years nor why, only whether the price is paid.

## Peoples and everyday life

Humans are numerous in Sâlvara. Sea Elves and Water Genasi live in coastal districts; Tiefling scribe families cluster around the vaults and courts; Dragonborn, Tortle, and Halfling communities work the cargo quays. No people is born bound to lifespan harvesting. The horror of Sâlvara is that the practice is not the product of one ancestry, but of state law.

Daily life is shaped by age counts. A child’s birthday is not only a celebration but a record compared with the security in the family vault. The wealthy use cosmetic magic to conceal age lines; in workers’ districts, greying hair signals poverty or the threat of harvest. The city’s common wish is not “live long,” but “may your years remain yours.”

## SD 83: empty vault, full palace

The council’s three oldest houses demand transfers at the same time. Vault physicians and harbour healers warn that storage seals are failing and the years could rebound at lethal speed during transfer. At that moment Umbraval voids a shadow contract held by a Sâlvaran house. As war captives disappear between the two states’ records, the city begins to ask whether the vaults are actually empty.
