# Varkesh

<p class="lore-lead">Before a warship leaves Varkesh harbour, two separate tables decide. Banner Command writes where it will go, why, and with what force; the Treasury Council calculates whether it can still return three months later with pay, provisions, and repairs. Without one seal, the other’s decision does not reach the sea.</p>

Varkesh is an independent island-city on the largest island of a three-island group between Nareth and Odraka. From outside it is known for calm, clear shores and level beaches. Inland, the island hardens: hills rise behind the coves, and the great mountain facing Nareth was quarried in the First Age, leaving the broad level ground on which the city stands.

The cut shaped more than Varkesh’s outline. Above, the city is a dense coastal settlement of narrow lanes, courtyard houses, tile roofs, arched stone façades, and stairs descending to the water. Below, Quietwater Harbour holds depots, provision basins, and shipyards. The same city keeps the account of war and watches warships in front of it; its two-key order exists so neither may sit in one hand.

## The island and its three shores

Varkesh’s northern slope is still called the **Split Face** for the old quarry marks. Extraction ended long ago; narrow terraces, rain cisterns, watch paths, and cliff gardens now cut through the wound. Upper City preserves cypress courtyards from its old shore gardens and stone squares open to the wind.

To the east, **Quietwater** is a natural harbour where long shallows break the sea so that even heavy cargo ships barely roll. **Three Shores** in the south opens toward the other two large islands and the smaller rocks; fishers, undersea guides, and short-route barges live there. **Windgate** faces open water to the west. Its walls, signal towers, and fleet quays receive military ships, while merchant vessels are kept in Quietwater whenever possible.

That division makes Varkesh prosperous in peace. In war it holds the whole city tense: closing one harbour may protect the other, and starve half the island.

## The two-key order

Varkesh’s two institutions did not begin apart. The island-state once had one council, one treasury, and harbour garrisons. During the continental wars, the public treasury could no longer meet army wages and imported grain at once. An emergency supply office turned merchant notes, quarter ration shares, and foreign pledges into one common ledger. When the war ended, merchants and soldiers trusted that ledger’s promises more than the state coin.

Today’s **Treasury Council** is that office made permanent. Foreign credit, grain and ammunition contracts, mercenary payroll, public debt, and major rescue funds pass through it. **Banner Command** oversees fleet, coast defence, shipyards, garrison, and expeditions abroad. Both use a system of examination, service, and term open to citizens of every people; no person may serve in both at once.

They are not two states. Varkesh has one law, one citizenship, and one harbour tax. But the following actions cannot take force without both keys:

- opening war credit to a foreign crown or claimant;
- sending armed forces beyond the island;
- releasing extraordinary treasury or grain reserves;
- granting supply, repair, or refuge to foreign warships;
- borrowing against long-term tax revenue.

Banner Command cannot carry every profitable war into Varkesh harbour. The Treasury Council cannot guarantee a ruler’s debt and use the fleet as that ruler’s private force. The arrangement is not peaceful. A bank can calculate a war’s return but cannot ignore an enemy fleet off the coast; an army wants to move against every threat but cannot choose the port from which its wages will come.

## Credit, war, and neutrality

Varkesh became famous not by growing ordinary citizens’ money, but where war’s material needs exceed what one state can carry alone. A crown can pledge future customs income, a dynasty a salt mine, or a city a share of three future harvests, and receive grain, repair, arms material, or military pay from Varkesh.

The Treasury Council stores less gold than it seeks a **campaign that can be repaid**. Its debt records therefore know not only which ruler is rich, but which harbour can endure a siege, which garrison has delayed wages, and which dynasty has lost the power to collect tax.

For Varkesh, a debtor’s defeat is not always a loss. The Council may transfer a pledged security to another claimant, or settle with the new government that agrees to recognise an old debt. The island is accused of causing wars. Varkeshi answer more narrowly: war began long before it reached their records; they make clear whose provisions will allow it to continue.

## Peoples and quarter life

No one people founded Varkesh, and no people owns either institution. Khoravar sea houses carried language, kinship, and trade ties to different coasts. Vedalken clerks formed one of the first generations maintaining common measures and terms during the war. Gargoyle families long held the night towers, stonework, and cliff paths of the Split Face. Kender quarters grew around small cargo, news, and family businesses at Three Shores. Simic hybrids are strong in Quietwater’s maintenance tunnels, diving crews, and salvage yards. Kithkin courtyards are the centres of quarter networks that kept food sharing alive during war.

These are not innate duties, but settled work shaped by who carried which risks at different points in island history. A Gargoyle may join the Treasury Council and a Kithkin Banner Command. Varkesh law protects that movement precisely because either institution becoming one people’s property would end the two-key order.

## Layers of the city

| --- | --- |
| **Upper City** | Council courts, old mansions, steep narrow lanes, and public record houses on the quarried plateau. |
| **Treasury Steps** | Grain stores, credit courts, inns for ship creditors, and great cisterns. |
| **Quietwater** | Merchant quays, fish market, repair basins, and Simic diving yards. |
| **Windgate** | Fleet quays, outer walls, barrack courts, and signal towers. |
| **Three Shores** | Short-route barges, open market, Kender courtyards, and travellers bound for the other islands. |
| **Split Face** | Old quarry terraces, night towers, rain paths, and mountain gardens. |

## 83 AS: the war on credit

The Treasury Council is currently weighing credit requests from two continental rulers seeking the same sea corridor. Banner Command does not treat the request as ordinary commerce: both sides have sent scouting vessels around Windgate, and it argues that the negotiations now directly endanger island defence.

The issue is not only whom to credit. If the Council refuses one side, that ruler may turn to the secret networks of the Mist Isles or harsher terms in another harbour. If it agrees, Varkesh ties its neutrality to a promissory note. For the first time, island citizens are asking openly whether two keys stop a war—or merely make it more orderly and more expensive.
