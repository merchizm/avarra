# River Throne

<p class="lore-lead">This matriarchal elective monarchy defines its border by watershed, not by land. Source, tributary, delta, and floodplain are parts of the same political body.</p>

<div class="lore-grid"><div class="lore-card"><h3>Capital</h3><p>Namaris</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Current situation</h3><p>As the currents slow, Queen Samara’s body weakens as well.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Places</h3><p>Three Mouths, Reedcrown, Blue Ladder, Akıntısız</p></div></div>

## Internal politics

Queen Samara’s illness and the slowing river fleet have made water-basin sovereignty an urgent political question. The court, river crews, and local basin authorities disagree over who may speak for the waters when the ruler’s body no longer seems separate from their flow.

## Foreign relations

Kar-Dur, the Thorn Crown, the Coral Palaces, and Ordan all affect the River Throne’s water politics. Ordan’s demand for harbour access is especially fraught. Source waters, watershed crossings, and the Three Mouths transport network make every border dispute a question of survival rather than map lines.

## Economy and transport

Namaris Scales, transport through Three Mouths, grain, salt, and ore bind the River Throne to continental prices. When currents falter, shipments and credit do as well. The realm’s economic strength lies in moving goods along the basin, making its present failure of flow a political crisis.
