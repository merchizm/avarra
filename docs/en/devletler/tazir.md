# Free City of Tazir

<p class="lore-lead">Tazir is an old stone city where the road from the coast reaches deep cisterns one last time before entering the dry interior. Its human-majority population defines freedom as everyone knowing their water share and maintenance duty in the open.</p>

The land around Tazir is not city land. Its authority ends at the gate, the cisterns, and the narrow road that connects them to the coast. Beyond that, the route, pasture, and passage law of nomadic communities applies. Respecting this boundary is a principal reason Tazir has endured for centuries.

## The water share

Every household holds a **water share**, a public record of how much water it may take from which cistern for drinking, gardens, animals, and emergency fire. A share may be inherited, but cannot be accumulated by one household or sold. If a household does not use its share, the drought council may temporarily transfer it to another district.

The **Cistern Council** keeps these records. Human districts hold most seats, but seasonal representatives of passing Centaur, Leonin, Halfling, and Orc communities have a voice in decisions about wells and passage beyond the city. Tazir treats this arrangement not as hospitality, but as mutual necessity.

## An old free city

Tazir’s founding stories differ. Some families say the first cistern was opened by human workers from the coast; others say an older local well agreement only became permanent behind stone walls. Everyone agrees that no dynasty has held a crown here for long. Two earlier rulers who closed the water road to their own soldiers were exiled by common decision of the districts.

For this reason Tazir distrusts outside offers of “protection.” A king’s garrison, a merchant’s private well, and a permanent city fence are seen as different forms of the same threat.

## Districts and economy

**Four Cisterns** contains the governing courts and main reservoirs. **Dry Market** is the shaded bazaar where coastal salt, leather, grain, textiles, and animals from migration routes are exchanged. **Road House** is an open yard where every large caravan entering the city stays for three days; travellers pay no tax, but promise to follow water and safety rules.

Tazir’s wealth lies not in possessing surplus water, but in knowing exactly when water will run out. Scribes, cistern engineers, and weather watchers therefore receive as much respect as warriors.

## SD 83: the unsealed well

An inland well opens onto a new water vein absent from the Cistern Council’s records. Northern merchants immediately want warehouses and walls around it; route communities call that an attack on passage law. For the first time, Tazir must choose between sharing water and guarding the power created by finding it.
