# Free City of Nasima

<p class="lore-lead">Nasima is an old, human-majority free city on the dry coast east of Umbraval. Its life does not depend on broad fields or conquered territory, but on knowing the shallow shore, tidal pockets, and safe anchorages that change with every season.</p>

Nasima was not founded as any crown’s colony. Human sailor families, coastal fishers, and caravan communities from inland settled the same coves over centuries. The city became less a dynasty’s property than a shared law of harbour labour. Human citizens are numerous, but citizenship comes from coastal duty, residence, and open surety, not ancestry.

## The coastal commons

Nasima’s shoreline cannot be sold. Piers, tidal pools, fishing grounds, and storm shelters are commons maintained by particular households on behalf of the city. A family may use the same anchorage for three generations, but cannot deny it to another ship during a storm. This rule is the basis of both the city’s freedom and its trade.

The **Coast Council** includes representatives sent by fishing districts, pilots, dock workers, inland-road merchants, and water wardens. It is not a permanent sitting body: major decisions are made when the tide turns, on days when everyone can reach the harbour. Outsiders call this slow; citizens say it prevents one family from rushing the city into war.

## The shadow of Slave Bay

[Sâlvara](./salvara) and [Umbraval](./umbraval-serbest-sehri) are visible on Nasima’s sea horizon. Nasima trades with both, but forbids the registration within its walls of goods born from lifespan harvesting or shadow leasing. The ban cannot keep slave labour wholly off the coast: false cargo records, freed captives, and families fleeing debt reach Nasima constantly.

This is the city’s harshest dispute. Some demand that ships carrying prohibited goods be turned away entirely. Pilot houses argue that such a decision would leave all coastal trade to Sâlvara and Umbraval.

## City life

**Low Lantern** is an old lighthouse on a narrow rock spur, with the houses of pilots around it. **Salt Market** is the covered meeting place for fish, salt, rope, dried fruit, and caravan news. **Return Courts** are neighbourhood squares where the names of the dead and missing at sea are read aloud; the final record of citizenship is kept there.

Human families do not claim coastal life as a single origin. Sea Elves, Water Genasi, Hadozee mastwrights, and Tortle travellers live in the city’s old districts. The human majority claims not a right to rule over this mixture, but an obligation to keep the harbour open.

## SD 83: the wrong anchorage

A ship from Slave Bay enters Nasima’s common shelter during a storm. Its manifest says salt, but year-share seals and the names of shadowless people are found beneath its deck. If the Coast Council holds the ship, it risks commercial retaliation from two powerful neighbours; if it releases it, Nasima’s own harbour law collapses.
