# Frost Throne

<p class="lore-lead">The Frost Throne is an absolute monarchy founded to govern the root threshold at Shadowless Glass and the past that returns from ice, built on the native order of Giants and Goliaths. For the Throne, preservation does not mean ownership; it means refusing to thaw something before it can survive change.</p>
<div class="lore-grid"><div class="lore-card"><h3>Capital</h3><p>[Nivarra](../yerlesimler/nivarra), a glacier fissure, fjord, and the archive of Frozen Witnesses.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>State duty</h3><p>The Winter Seal: threshold safety, frozen testimony, and open northern routes.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Foreign policy</h3><p>Supports Prince Odran and seeks a Lethar marriage alongside controlled access to northern trade.</p></div><div class="lore-card"><h3>Places</h3><p>White Bridge, Shadowless Glass, Last Hearth, and Nivarra’s ice archives.</p></div></div>

## Why does this state exist?

The Frost Throne formed after the Veil Wars, when it became clear that the ice around Shadowless Glass was more than ordinary winter ice. It slowed the signs of the living root threshold and prevented the gate from being used at the convenience of any army. The Closure required that no ruler treat the gate as a road, tax station, or military supply line. The Throne was created to uphold that prohibition, then gradually turned temporary stewardship into a permanent claim to rule.

That is why it is not the court of one people. Elven houses that settled later hold influence over threshold rhythm and gate law. Frost Genasi manage thaw calendars and the safety of documents and bodies recovered from ice. Goliaths keep the ice roads, evacuation order, and physical maintenance of the fissures alive. Shadar-kai households preserve oral testimony for oaths older than writing, Rimekin communities bring light and winter-shelter knowledge, and fjord peoples provide food and sea access. The Throne needs all of them; its government does not mistake that necessity for equal representation.

## The Winter Seal

The Winter Seal consists of three joined obligations. First, it does not simply keep Shadowless Glass closed: it ensures the threshold cannot open without the right time, a local keeper, and a recognised oath, as required by the Root Closure. Second, it decides under which era’s law treaties, dead, criminal evidence, and people emerging from Nivarra’s ice will be heard. Third, it keeps northern routes serving survival rather than trade in times of storm or war.

Those duties make the Throne indispensable, but also create its central danger. Declaring a document “not yet ready to thaw” can delay a family’s claim or an accounting for war crimes for centuries. Closing a road for “winter safety” can starve villages. The Frost Throne’s fear of thaw is not a seasonal temperament; it is the politics of a regime that knows reopening the past may also dissolve its own legitimacy.

## Powers beneath the throne

The **Chamber of Stillness** contains the Winter Throne’s jurists and guardians, who regard change as lawful only when proclaimed by the crown. The **Thaw Calendar** is a network of mostly Frost Genasi archivists who demand public records, independent witnesses, and citizenship guarantees for every recovery. The **Burden Oath** joins Goliath road keepers and fjord granaries through mutual cargo and evacuation contracts; it is powerful enough to halt a war convoy, yet too dependent to confront the crown openly. The **Unwritten Witnesses** is the loose name for Shadar-kai households that insist a written guest oath cannot erase older words.

The throne holds these powers in consultation, not shared sovereignty. The Frost Throne’s central political question is therefore not “when does ice melt?” It is “who owns what emerges, and whose word will be believed?”

## Root-gate policy

The Palace calls the bond at Shadowless Glass “a threshold protected under silence.” Passage requires a winter witness, the consent of a local keeper, and a written guest oath; none is sufficient alone. Shadar-kai households say some oaths are older than writing, while the Thaw Calendar argues that the Palace has turned a temporary safety measure into a permanent monopoly. As the ice thaws, the real conflict is not when the gate should fall silent, but whether the Palace still has the right to silence it.

## Table tensions

- A witness recovered from ice claims the founding oath of the Frost Throne includes a clause that violates the Root Closure.
- The Thaw Calendar wants to release evidence of a war crime; the Chamber of Stillness says it will drive the fjords into civil war.
- The Burden Oath opens a sealed route for a starving village. Will the Palace call it rescue, or complicity in a crime against the state?
