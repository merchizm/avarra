# Ordan Empire

Ordan ideology holds that unmanaged nature produces famine and monsters. Rivers, forests, and peoples must be measured, recorded, and bound into state order. The empire brings roads, granaries, and courts to conquered places; in return, it demands taxes, soldiers, and cultural conformity.

This ideology is a selective reading of the [Seven Lines Refuge](../tarih/yedi-hat-siginagi). Measure and record began there to make aid visible during disaster; in the empire, they became the language of conquest and control.

## Succession candidates

- **General Varessa:** Backed by the army; advocates occupying the Thorn Crown.
- **Prince Odran:** Adopted heir, allied with merchants and the bureaucracy.
- **High Priest Maelor:** Backed by nobles and the state religion; advocates Veyran supremacy.

## Internal politics

Varessa’s base is the army and its frontier officers. Odran depends on merchant houses, officials, and those who need an orderly succession. Maelor draws support from nobles and the state faith. Their conflict is not simply personal: it pits conquest, administrative continuity, and Veyran supremacy against one another.

## Foreign relations

The Thorn Crown is the most immediate target of Ordan’s expansion, but diplomacy with Lethar, Kar-Dur, and the River Throne is equally consequential. The Nine Lanterns, in particular, sit within a wider contest over routes, underground access, and sovereign records. Every treaty is shadowed by the question of which claimant can promise roads, grain, and military force.

## Economy and routes

Nareth grain, credit at Forty Bridges, the authority of the Ordan Seal, and the imperial road network keep the empire moving. These systems make campaigns possible, but also spread risk: disruption to grain, debt, or bridges can become a succession crisis before a battle is fought.
