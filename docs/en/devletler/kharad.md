# Free City of Kharad

<p class="lore-lead">Kharad is an old free city roughly two hundred kilometres inland from the coast, at the pass where dry canyons send sea-road caravans onto interior routes. Desert surrounds it; rock-cut cisterns, shaded courts, and pass watches sustain its life.</p>

Kharad does not claim to rule tribes, pastures, or routes beyond its boundary. Anyone wishing to cross the pass accepts the city’s well and watch rules, while Kharad law ends beyond it. This limited sovereignty is both the city’s pride and its safeguard.

## Citizenship of the pass

In Kharad, citizenship is earned through watch more than birth. Each adult citizen serves a period in cistern maintenance, pass watch, caravan rescue, or city stores. Once that service is complete, the person gains a vote in the district council and the right to testify before the city court.

The **Pass Council** represents districts, but the watch houses that protect the cisterns and narrow road carry separate weight in it. This makes Kharad the harshest, most military, and most unequal of the four free cities. Even so, the council explicitly forbids any house from establishing hereditary rule: if the key to the pass remains in one hand, the whole city can go thirsty.

## The market of stone

**Broken Step** is the outer terrace where caravans from the coast rest. **Shadow Wells** are hollowed districts of water stores and night markets. **High Cleft** holds watchtowers, rope bridges, and the stone homes of pass sentries. Kharad lives by stonework, leather, pack animals, metal repair, and road guiding.

Humans are numerous in the city, but Kharad knows it cannot survive without recognising the voice that Centaur, Leonin, Orc, Halfling, and other route peoples hold over the pass. Its idea of freedom is not to resemble mobile peoples, but not to treat their road as property.

## Old city, new pressure

No one knows when Kharad’s first stone wall was raised. Its own records say the oldest cisterns predate the city and that the first council was created only to stop anyone from closing them. This story begins Kharad’s history not with a dynasty, but a shared fear of thirst.

Today the city fears the coastal slave trade reaching interior routes. Sâlvaran buyers seek a safe canyon road for year-shares; some Kharad merchants think it will enrich the city. The watchers answer that such a road will carry not only goods, but human hunting.

## SD 83: the open pass

An old stone road collapses above the canyon, forcing every caravan toward Kharad’s narrow main gate. The Pass Council wants to limit passage rights; nomadic communities call it an unlawful closure. Amid the crowd, Sâlvaran merchants are found to have brought armed men to guard convoys carrying year-shares.
