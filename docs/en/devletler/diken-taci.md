# Thorn Crown

The Thorn Crown is not a state built in a forest, but a legal order that recognises the forest itself as a sovereign being. Land cannot be sold; groves, valleys, and hunting grounds may only be used for fixed terms. A major construction needs the ruler’s approval and that of the druids who interpret the local root network.

Queen Elyra Thornsoul is slowly becoming a living tree because of a treaty made forty years ago. She can speak less and less, yet has still not named her successor.

## Root-gate politics

The Echo-Root bond around the Throne Tree is not property of the Thorn Crown, but a sovereign witness. The Root Guardians argue that the gate may open only with the grove’s consent, a living threshold, and a recognised oath. The Open Branch Council seeks limited passage for refugees and trade. Elyra’s transformation has turned the question of which word remains binding into a succession crisis.

## Communities and groups

- **Crown Elves:** Influential in foreign policy, archives, and major settlements.
- **Root Firbolgs:** Protect sacred places, migrations, and funerary groves; remain distant from central government.
- **Thornbloods:** An ancient humanoid community, the army’s backbone, which demands equal citizenship.
- **Underbranch Gnomes:** Manage under-root passages, seed archives, and communication networks.
- **Root Guardians / Open Branch Council:** The principal blocs contesting human removal and limited settlement rights.

## Internal politics

The Root Guardians, Open Branch Council, Red Hunt, Pale Circle, and Hornless Army disagree over whether the Crown must seal itself, accept limited human settlement, or prepare for war. Each claims to protect the forest in a different way. As Elyra’s transformation proceeds, no faction can agree on who has the authority to interpret the final terms of her treaty or select a successor.

## Foreign relations

Ordan’s pressure and the prospect of invasion dominate Thorn Crown diplomacy. Tensions with Lethar, Kar-Dur, the River Throne, and Wildhearth are shaped by forest crossings, refugees, water routes, and old claims over roads and settlements. None of these relations is cleanly hostile or friendly: every neighbour is also a route, a refuge, or a potential claimant in the throne crisis.

## Economy and routes

The Crown imports metal, salt, and grain. It exports timber, fibre, medicinal plants, and seed stock under term-bound harvesting law. The Thorn-Meridian Road is more than a trade route: it is a military and political pressure line between forest forts and Ordan’s wider road network.
