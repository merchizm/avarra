# Meridian Age

<p class="lore-lead">The Meridian Age is not the planned rise of Ordan; it is the age in which the plan was written afterward. One measure city slid from arbitration to patronage and from patronage to empire, each step carried by telling a different people a different excuse.</p>

All the great rivers of Asteron share one name: Meridian, and the age takes its name from the first Meridian Hospitality. The name is linked to an old Veyran water-law term meaning “measured flow”; its origin is disputed. The landings on the Nareth coast in 96 BC stalled in forest and mountain; the empire turned inward and between 82 and 18 BC took Asteron’s human kingdoms one by one. The Fifth Kingdom, squeezed between Ordan and the coast, remained on the edge of the Drift.

Official records credit all of it to a single “Meridian Plan.” This page also sets beside it the accounts that say the plan was written later.

## One Measure City Among Many · 350–320 BC

Ordanor was not yet an imperial capital, only one of many Veyran measure cities. The Veyran cities around it had been weakened by war, drought, unsafe roads, and legal systems that did not match. Ordanor’s advantage was not military: river roads, grain districts, and a crossing of two land routes made its records grow faster than the others’. Its neighbours chose it as arbiter not because it was strongest, but because it was least feared.

The first arbitrations were small: a common grain scale, a road-guard share, a border water dispute. Some cities did not see this as a first step toward handing authority to a record council; some saw it and refused. The refusers would learn years later that they depended on its grain standard.

## The Guest Throne · 340–300 BC

In these years the River Throne was a state in Nareth with no land of its own. Its mixed people—humans, Halflings, Lizardfolk, Loxodon—lived at the edge of elf and dwarf sovereignty under an unwritten **guest oath**, renewed with every new ruler. Dwarves held the headwaters; elf houses held the riverbank groves. When the Throne said “the whole current is one body,” the head and feet of that body stood on other people’s land.

The tension was old: elf groups said the Throne’s dams and canals damaged the root network, and dwarves quarrelled over their share of the sources. Veyran water arbiters first appeared in this dispute. They were neutral, they kept written records, and as payment for arbitration they asked the right to copy both sides’ flood and share records. Nobody objected; copies seemed a small thing.

## The Same Words, Different Ears · 320–290 BC

Over the next twenty years the Veyran arbiters told each party a different story, and none of the stories matched.

To the elf groups they showed that canals were harming the root network; the proof was the Throne’s own flood records. In return for the damaged groves they promised settlement and record protection in Asteron. To the dwarves they offered a knowledge exchange: the passage layouts of Vein stations, knowledge most dwarves had lost or never written; Veyran families held it in memory and ledger copies, because the stations had been those families’ old workplace. At the same table the old ledgers of dependent labour were mentioned: the history of the Nimrûn, Myconids, and Duergar lay in those ledgers. No arbiter said it as a threat.

To the Throne they spoke only of security, trade, and union. The dwarves already wanted the Throne gone, and so did part of the elves. The Veyrans supplied the excuses that persuaded them to do what they already wished.

## The Match Kept Dry · 300–280 BC

Then the people intervened. The first fire started on a river barge; a quarrel between an elf villager and a Throne riverbank farmer flared for a reason still unknown. A few months later Throne villagers closed a river branch; elf groves flooded. Water to the dwarf hearths was cut off. Each side said the other started it.

The Veyrans handed nobody a match; they only kept the match dry. Insurance for burned barges was paid only to boatmen registered in Ordanor. Rumours left the arbiters’ table and spread quietly. The Throne’s proverb began to be said in these years: *A guest is a guest until they leave the host’s sight.*

## The Meridian Hospitality · 280–250 BC

The Ordanor Record Council offered the Throne a way out: lodging on the Meridian rivers. The Throne was entered in a legal **Guest Ledger** as an “honoured guest.” In return the Veyrans asked for access: quays, locks, granaries, and a measure house at every bend. They asked for trading rights and “political unity”; a common River Council would be formed.

Some terms of the agreement were strange, and no reason was given. No boat could anchor in the lakes of the Seven Ravines. The Veyrans asked for the right to take measurements at night. The Throne’s keeper households were to settle on the ravine shores. The Throne had no other path; it accepted.

## The River Ledger · 270–230 BC

Asteron’s local peoples did not welcome the Throne. The Independent Quay Councils wanted to know how water and passage rights would be shared. The Veyrans did not present a single plan; they gave each people a different currency.

Land and bank stayed local; the current went to the Throne. The **River Ledger** read each community’s rights aloud before two witnesses and corrected them. Local people found the ledger honest if not sincere, because the Veyrans’ own past was one of being unrecorded. Kalashtar became neutral arbiters at Düş Eşiği through dream mediation. Vedalken at Cam Kanat merged flood records with the Throne’s archive. Giff took on roads and haulage. The debts of Gnome trading cities were erased; in return they ran locks and ferries. Hobgoblin frontier units became river guards. The children of local leaders were sent to Ordanor schools.

Mistakes were made in the first decade. A flood nearly ended the agreement; two valleys refused, and kept refusing. The “single plan” story was written later by the court.

## The Ravine School · 260–180 BC

The closed session of the Ordanor Record Council founded a school publicly known as the “Water School.” It was said to train hydraulic engineers and arbiters. In reality the school stood on the shore of the Seven Ravines.

Children of the inner circle stayed here for years and were “tuned.” In some, the signs deepened: a new iris ring, sight in darkness, blindness to light. Some were tuned and returned; some did not. School records sometimes note that a child “forgot their name”; no explanation is written beside it. Veyran families regarded the school as a privilege.

One sentence recurs in the closed session’s minutes: *No one will be lost.* No record explains what the sentence means.

## The Great Correction · 230–200 BC

Two generations later the share corrections in the River Ledger were totalled. Thousands of small corrections, made under the principle that “correction is common work,” had added up, always in Ordanor’s favour. It was called the **Great Correction**.

Was it intent, accumulated error, or the betrayal of a few arbiter families? Nobody knows. Three revolts followed. The Independent Quay Councils struck at the quays; Ordan placated them with debt relief. Anti-Order Veyrans broke away in rural valleys: for them the River Ledger was the second form of the Guest Ledger, and the language of the old station ledgers, which counted marked children as instruments, had returned. Within the Throne, one house broke its oath of loyalty; Ordan silenced it by granting new seats. The first civil war was put down this way.

## Census, Road, and Emperor · 200–100 BC

In this age the empire took form as an apparatus. Standard roads, public granaries, record towers, and regular censuses were built. Some peoples were resettled from their homes into new regions; for some it meant work, for others exile. The bureaucracy chosen by examination opened to conquered local nobles. The Unlined and Border Veyrans rose through that door. Hobgoblin military traditions entered the professionalization of the army.

Around 120 BC the first emperor was crowned and the **Imperial Reckoning** began. Palace historians rewrote the past in these years: accidents and hasty moves became parts of one destiny called the “Meridian Plan.” There was no fixed rule of succession; an emperor chose an adopted heir.

## An Attempt to Break Out · 96–83 BC

Ordan first tried to go outward. Forts were built on the Nareth coast on the grounds of trade and fighting piracy; Vekra Port became the main naval base of the expeditions. Advancing inland from the coast was not possible. In the forest, supply roads rotted and maps were useless; Veyran cartographers could not tie the trees’ positions to a fixed line. In the mountains, Kar-Dur’s hearth councils were not organized in a way that could be gathered under one command and made to surrender. The coastal forts remained; not one step further was taken.

## The Turn Inward: Five Kingdoms · 82–18 BC

Blocked in Nareth, the empire turned inward and took Asteron’s human kingdoms.

In the Strait Wars of 82–75 BC, the Broken Tower tried to stop Ordan ships from reaching the inland river routes; the human kingdoms could not form a common fleet. In 74 BC its coastal forts fell. One branch of the dynasty made terms with Ordan; the others fled to Nareth and mixed with nomadic herds there. The origin of the split between conciliatory and martial factions in today’s Broken Tower House lies here.

In 63–58 BC Ordan cut the grain roads around Halvek. The High Crown’s nobles levied heavy taxes and soldiers from the people; the city guilds turned against the court. In 57 BC the gates of Halvek were opened from within. Ordan forbade looting and granted rights to the guilds that opened the gates. Human historians still debate whether it was betrayal or deliverance.

In 49–43 BC the White Hearth was annexed after a long defence; much of its people crossed the sea to the Thorn Crown. In 38–31 BC the remaining human kingdoms tried to choose a common high ruler; the dynasties could not agree on authority and succession. In 31–18 BC the Kingdom of Qam was not conquered directly; dynastic war, floods, plague, salinization, and the cutting of trade tore it apart. Its land was divided among the River Throne, Ordan-backed local rulers, and independent marsh communities. The Throne thus grew by Ordan’s hand.

## Ends Thought Closed · 60–18 BC

On paper Asteron was united. In practice three ends never closed. In the north, Lethar remained a pole no emperor dared attack, with the customs of Telor Gate and the Star Veil. The River Throne kept its basin law against the standard of the River Office. A few valleys never fully submitted.

Ordan managed these ends by bargaining rather than closing them. Palace records call all of them “closed.”

## Two Walls of Nareth · 96–0 BC

In Nareth only dwarves and elves remained sovereign; humans lived as nomadic herds. The Thorn Crown halted Ordan with its legal order that treats the forest as a sovereign being. The Dikenkanlı served in those wars; their claims to full citizenship became an internal matter for later centuries. Refugees from the White Hearth and fugitives from the Broken Tower were counted as **temporary guests**. Geyikgeçidi became the military centre where the roads to the Ordan border met.

Kar-Dur’s hearth councils noticed Ordan’s interest in the ancient dwarven gates. The first signs of the Oathsworn–Openers division appeared in those years. By the rule of the Root Closure no state could open a gate alone; the rule turned Ordan’s gate claims into a political argument.

## The Poles of the North · 350–0 BC

The Lethar Star Kingdom was small but frightening to attack: a star defence on nine terraces, centuries of magical training, and elite forces that could gather under one command. Ordan saw it not as a “province to take” but as an independent pole that, if its passes were closed, could cut the empire’s northern artery. The customs of Telor Gate became a constant point of friction between them.

The Frost Throne was founded to guard the root threshold in Gölgesiz Cam. It rejected Ordan’s gate registers: *a gate is not a ledger item*. Household marriages in the Miravel gardens tightened its tie to Lethar.

## The Underground · 350–0 BC

The Dokuz Fener Union defended its Nimrûn Svirfneblin cities with sound law and deceptive tunnels. Trade with Ordan was controlled; when Ordan’s road engineering met the Fener’s sound law in the same tunnel, neither recognized the other. Kor-Dum, with its worker and master councils, and Black Anvil, with its noble chain monarchy, carried the same Duergar past in opposite ways. Dark Harbor grew into a free underground harbour where even warring sides traded.

## Shadows of the Record Order · 300–0 BC

The more Ordan wanted everything entered in record and measure, the more states found their role selling the opposite. The Sis Adaları grew strong on false identity, debt, and insurance; Umbraval on shadow registers and contract hire. The Black Seal Isles built a chain of weights, assay, and seals not tied to Ordan’s ruler. Varkesh and the Free City of Winter Granaries turned the supply of the expeditions into credit. The Sunmeadow Union kept its law of debt forgiveness and refuge. All of these states are both against the empire’s record order and fed by it.

## Odraka and Qasyrra · 350–0 BC

In Odraka the right of passage remained more important than land ownership. The salt basins in the west produced a good indispensable to the empire’s granary order. Qasyrra kept its interior closed while continuing to sell spice and dye to the outside world; Ordan’s treasury grew more and more dependent on them. The coastal cities of the Bay of Slaves found a new market in the empire’s need for labour to build roads and granaries.

## Season-States and the Fey Thread · 350–0 BC

Altıngüneş and the Bakır Principalities continued to live by their own oaths despite the “dangerous passage” classification of Ordan’s records.

Ordan’s record order again classified root thresholds as “dangerous passages” and “unregistered oaths.” But the Root Closure prevented the empire from rebuilding Damar’s old control: a tower or seal cannot replace a living threshold and words recognized on the Fey side. Every official claim to a gate is therefore a political dispute for local keepers and court witnesses.

## The Last Census · 18–0 BC

Ordan’s last great census began in 18 BC. The Fifth Kingdom was the last human kingdom the empire had not taken; squeezed against the coast, it stood between Ordan and the sea. The campaign calendar was ready. Granaries were full, roads were open.

> **Year 18, harvest month.** The final campaign calendar against the Fifth Kingdom entered into the record.
>
> **Year 11.** The envoy returned. The king swore the oath. The border records were handed over.
>
> **Year 3, winter.** The census officer reread the kingdom’s border records. They were consistent.
>
> **Year 1, last month.** Record: Kingdom of [          ]. Ruler: [          ]. Border: [          ].
>
> **Year 0.**
>
> [no record]

Ordan’s record towers counted nothing that night. The campaign order was left without a target.

### No One Was Asleep

The official record makes that night quiet. The silence was only at the centre; everyone around it noticed something, and none could say what had gone missing.

**The border and the army.** In the years before the census, farms near the coast told odd stories: horses halted at a certain line, birds flew past the shore and turned. Ordan clerks logged it as “coast sickness” and moved on. The real worry was the Fifth Kingdom’s lack of resistance. It strengthened no fortress and asked no one for help; its envoy came back empty-handed, and though its king had sworn the oath, its neighbours took the docility for a trap. The River Throne’s civilian observers wrote “too docile” in their ledgers; Ordan’s staff officers avoided using the same words twice. The campaign army waited, ready and tense, because no campaign had ever looked so easy.

**The mages.** In the census year, Lethar’s Star Veil marked a gap in the star records above the eastern shore: its masters counted the sky twice in one night and the counts did not agree. A written warning went to the Meridian court through the Telor Gate customs house; the court shelved it as “northern trade anxiety”. After the event Lethar’s healer-mages went down to the border. They did not sleep through it; the opposite happened: the magic worked and produced nothing. Healing did not renew, fire did not warm, a summoned light burned and did not illuminate. The Veil masters wrote that such complete practice had never before sat beside such complete ineffect. Lethar and the Stateless have not let go of the Fifth Kingdom’s trace since.

**The elves.** In the months before the census, the Thorn Crown’s Echo-Root bonds stopped answering toward the east. Root-wardens reported that news from the direction of the Basin first slowed, then began to repeat: the same news, in the same words, three times. The Thorn Crown wrote this to Ordan plainly; because Ordan had filed root thresholds under “unrecorded oath”, the letter was classified but never answered. The Pale Circle first met after that correspondence.

**The Ordan court.** When the campaign order was left without a target, the court believed for the first week that the record towers were faulty. By the second week the census clerks could say no name but “the eastern shore”. Vanguard columns marched to the coast and reported that the road turned back on itself, that they had crossed the same caravan track three times. Ordan sealed the area, declared finds state property, and had the old records read again: the records were complete; only the reader’s mind failed to keep what they said. The court never said “we were defeated”. The campaign entry was not closed; it was left suspended in the ledger.

**Those from the shore.** People from the coastal villages fled to Nareth and Asteron. None could name the place they came from; some families could not recall where one another had come from. The River Throne built its observation network on these people’s written testimony. The Kar-Dur hearth councils decided in those years to restrict the opening of ancient roads.

Everyone did something; none could show what it had been for. The six theories share this gap; the [Stagnation file](../durgunluk/) treats none of them as proven.

## Differences between accounts

Everyone tells the Meridian Hospitality as a different event, and each account carries a different lesson into the present. The Meridian palace record calls it an honoured guesting of a sister state, and concludes that Ordan’s rise was natural and planned. The River Throne chronicles tell the same event as the only way out for an expelled people; for them, guesting is a kind of captivity. The elf house accounts tie the matter to the dams: the Throne’s dams ruined the forest, and they had to protect their homeland. The dwarf hearth records read it as a knowledge exchange and say the Veyrans paid for their secrets.

For the Anti-Order movements, the Hospitality is the state form of the Guest Ledger; the empire betrayed and inverted the first refuge. The old Halvek guilds say something else entirely: they opened the gates themselves, and Ordan appeared to them as a deliverer.

None of these accounts cleanly refutes the others; each reads the same traces through its own interest. The real difference lies in who had to tell which story.
