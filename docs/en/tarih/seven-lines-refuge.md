# The Seven Lines Refuge

<p class="lore-lead">The Seven Lines Refuge was not a laboratory that “created” the Veyrans, nor a military order. It was a chain of refuges built by many generations of human communities who shared water, grain, roads, and responsibility after a great earthquake.</p>

## After the Breakage

Around 2640 BC, a great earthquake struck Asteron’s inner basins and drove communities into seven deep ravines and the safe ridges around them. The ravines later filled with water; old canals sometimes flowed backward, road markers shifted, and harvest calendars stopped agreeing. Seven settlements made a common protection system along high stone ridges, two river branches, and a surviving aqueduct. It was later called the **Seven Lines**.

The Lines were not a wall. Each was an open route joining water gates, grain stores, road stones, refuge courts, and watch houses. Every adult could be responsible not to a household but to water, bread, care, road, children, record, or watch. The first records were not perfect bureaucracy; they were an effort not to forget who was missing or in need.

## The emergence of the Veyrans

The refuge life endured for roughly three centuries. Children of families continually exposed to Vein water, stone dust, and unstable magical fields developed shared signs: two nested iris rings, fine natural lines on neck and hands, and sensitivity to changes in light. After the 2500s BC, these signs became widespread and heritable; neighbouring communities began to call these people **Veyran**.

Veyran identity is an inherited peoplehood, not an ordeal, illness, or military training gained later in life. In mixed families the signs may appear differently, and no sign alone determines culture or citizenship. Whether a Veyran is Ordan, border-born, migrant, rural, clerical, or anti-order comes from lived ties, not their body.

## The refuge’s inheritance

When the Seven Lines dispersed, no single state remained. Mill villages, market towns, road families, and water councils spread in different directions. Four principles endured:

- **Witness:** To prevent a person vanishing, at least two people should know their name, neighbour, and need.
- **Measure:** A scarce resource must be made visible before it can be shared fairly.
- **Rotation:** Watch and duty belong to rotating responsibility, not a permanent class.
- **Correction:** An incorrect record is common work, not a person’s failing.

Later Veyran cities turned these principles into schools, water law, examinations, and archive custom. Ordan especially took measure and record into the language of conquest, often pushing witness, rotation, and correction aside.

## Different accounts

| Account | What were the Seven Lines? | What it says today |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Meridian palace record** | First model of a chosen people meant to bring order | Veyran government looks natural and superior |
| **Border Veyran account** | People who did not abandon one another in disaster | No ancestry has a right to rule |
| **Anti-order movements** | A network for living with the world, not measuring it into submission | The empire reversed the refuge’s purpose |
| **Asteron academies** | Long environmental and magical transformation | Bodily difference is not social destiny |

None of these accounts conceals a secret “true origin”; each reads the same public historical traces through a different political meaning.
